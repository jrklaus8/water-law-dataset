## 1. Plain-language summary (about 200 words)

Many households live next to water pipes or sanitation services yet cannot use them, because of rules and paperwork: who counts as eligible, what documents or land title an application needs, what it costs, how much discretion officials have, and whether the rules are enforced. We call this the *legal last mile*. We searched the research literature and, with the help of an AI system working under a human researcher's direction, assessed what studies show about it. We included 1,080 studies from many countries, most published since 2010. Most describe situations and processes; only 63 used designs that could show that a rule *causes* a change in access. For 60 studies we could compare the direction of results, but the studies measured different things and we did not combine them into one number. Where results could be compared, being recognised or eligible usually went with better access, and help from administrators usually went with better access, while results for barriers and ownership pointed in different directions. Our confidence in any causal claim is low. The AI did most of the work and humans have checked only part of it, so these findings should be treated as a map of the evidence rather than as firm conclusions. [Authors: confirm and simplify.]

## 2. AI-use disclosure for the journal (draft paragraph)

This is an AI-assisted systematic review. A large language model (Claude, Anthropic) performed most of the title/abstract first-pass screening, full-text screening, data extraction, risk-of-bias appraisal and synthesis drafting, under the direction of the human researcher, who defined the question, protocol and eligibility criteria, ran the database searches, supplied the full texts and made the closing and scoping decisions. Independent human verification was partial: a human second reviewer covered the 3,665 title/abstract records the AI marked include or unsure (not the AI's exclusions), and confirmed 197 of the 1,080 full-text includes; no extracted value or risk-of-bias rating has been independently verified against source papers, and the researcher adjudicated about 175 further full-text decisions (open items A16 and A22 to A25) but the remaining AI full-text decisions have not been checked by a human. Model and tool provenance, as logged in the repository: Claude (Anthropic) made the title/abstract first-pass decisions (labels `Claude-AI-1stpass-2026-09-10` and `-2026-09-11`), the full-text decisions (labels `Claude-AI-fulltext-2026-09-12` to `Claude-AI-fulltext-2026-10-14`, plus 60 early rows labelled `claude_sonnet_5` and 519 legacy rows labelled `Claude` or `claude`) and the extraction and appraisal records (labels dated 2026-09-12 to 2026-10-14). The repository records a Claude model version only as the label `claude_sonnet_5` on those 60 rows and as "Claude Sonnet 5" in the second-reviewer tools disclosure for 2026-10-03 and 2026-10-04; for the other decisions only the stage and the date are logged, so the exact Claude model version behind them is not recorded.

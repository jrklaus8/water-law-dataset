## 1. Plain-language summary (about 200 words)

Many households live next to water pipes or sanitation services yet cannot use them, because of rules and paperwork: who counts as eligible, what documents or land title an application needs, what it costs, how much discretion officials have, and whether the rules are enforced. We call this the *legal last mile*. We searched the research literature and, with the help of an AI system working under a human researcher's direction, assessed what studies show about it. We included 1,159 studies from many countries, most published since 2010. Most describe situations and processes; only 68 used designs that could show that a rule *causes* a change in access. For 62 studies we could compare the direction of results, but the studies measured different things and we did not combine them into one number. Where results could be compared, being recognised or eligible usually went with better access, and help from administrators usually went with better access, while results for barriers and ownership pointed in different directions. Our confidence in any causal claim is low. The AI did most of the work and humans have checked only part of it, so these findings should be treated as a map of the evidence rather than as firm conclusions. [Authors: confirm and simplify.]

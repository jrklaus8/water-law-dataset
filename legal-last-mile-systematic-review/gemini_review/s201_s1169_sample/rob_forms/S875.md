## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This paper presents a mixed-methods external evaluation of the WaterAid-Bangladesh Urban Programme across informal settlements ('slums') in Dhaka and Chittagong, originally conducted in November 2001 by an anthropologist, a qualitative consultant, and a survey researcher, and summarized for publication by two non-fieldwork writers (including WaterAid's Programme Learning Facilitator; PDF p. 1). Appraised using the MMAT (2018) covering qualitative (1.1-1.5), quantitative non-randomized (3.1-3.5), and mixed-methods integration (5.1-5.5) criteria, the study's greatest methodological strength lies in its substantive integration (5.2-5.4: Yes). Pairing descriptive survey comparisons (N = 1,130: 491 beneficiary and 639 non-beneficiary households) with household case vignettes, field observations, and partner NGO staff interviews allows the authors to explain why high headline access to safe drinking water inside slums (98.4% of beneficiaries vs. 77.3% of non-beneficiaries; Table 2) coexists with severe exclusion of the 'poorest of the poor' from adequate daily water volumes, sanitary latrines, and management committees under a 100% cost-recovery mandate. However, per MMAT guidance that overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component, overall methodological concern is high. In the quantitative non-randomized strand, confounding is unaddressed beyond three-tier poverty cross-tabulations (3.4: No)—with self-selection into fee-paying user groups, selective NGO entry into better-off slums, exclusion of one partner's area (BAWPA) for security reasons, no baseline data, no inferential statistics, and an unexplained 491 vs. 639 sample split despite a stated equal-allocation rule (3.1, 3.3: Can't tell)—and major implementation deviations occurred (3.5: No). In the qualitative strand, third-party summarization omits interview counts, sampling frames, and coding/analysis procedures (1.2, 1.3, 1.5: Can't tell), resulting in non-adherence to full quality criteria across both traditions (5.5: No).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Zaunda et al. (2018) conduct a primary qualitative field assessment of disability-friendly school WASH facilities across all 10 primary schools (7 public, 3 private) in Rumphi town, northern Malawi, triangulating structured WHO (2009)-based facility observations and local adaptation market-cost estimates with interviews of 23 students with disabilities, 11 teachers, and 2 district officials. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits Moderate methodological concern (6 Yes, 2 Can't tell [Q5, Q8], 1 No [Q6], plus valuable research contribution on Q10). Key strengths include a complete town-wide school census covering both public and private providers, multi-stakeholder perspectives, and direct physical observations/measurements that revealed normalization of barriers by students and underestimation by teachers. Key methodological weaknesses include omitted interview guides and translation details alongside substantial student non-response during interviews (Q5), absence of researcher reflexivity regarding adult/authority-child power dynamics (Q6), unexplained reliance on teacher rather than parental written consent for day-school minors aged 6–18 (noted under Q7 despite national ethics approval), minimal one-sentence reporting of hand-coding procedures without coder/reliability details (Q8), and exclusion of parents and out-of-school children with disabilities.

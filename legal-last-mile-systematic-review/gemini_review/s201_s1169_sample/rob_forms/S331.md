## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Study S331 (Sarfo 2025) is a retrospective non-randomized comparative administrative study using Portage County Health District (PCHD, Ohio) enforcement records from January 2, 2015, to June 30, 2023 (n = 1,021 failed systems in descriptive tables; n = 938 in multivariable logistic regression) to evaluate whether Point-of-Sale (POS) real-estate transfer OWTS inspections increase timely compliance (<=120 days) with Board of Health Notices of Violation (BOH NOVs) compared with non-POS O&M inspections (annual Class I/NPDES aeration, 12-month new-system, nuisance complaint, and stormwater IDDE inspections). Appraised under ROBINS-I, overall risk of bias is Serious, driven by three Serious-risk domains (D1 Confounding, D2 Selection of participants, and D5 Missing data) and four Moderate-risk domains (D3, D4, D6, D7). Specifically: (1) the observational logistic regressions omit key socioeconomic and real-estate transaction confounders (household income, wealth, property value, sale liquidity, and baseline failure severity) while inappropriately adjusting for three post-inspection remediation variables (new system type, repair vs. replacement, and correction cost) that lie downstream of inspection context; (2) complete-case restriction to 938 of 1,021 failed OWTSs with non-missing remediation type/cost data (alongside 46 excluded 'voluntary' failures and 481 initially excluded records) risks conditioning sample selection on post-exposure remediation progress; and (3) the dissertation contains an unresolved legal contradiction regarding its own study site, stating in Chapter 2 (p. 24) that Portage County's POS program is voluntary and does not halt property sales upon failure, yet claiming in Chapter 5 (p. 84) that Portage County regulations mandate pre-transfer POS inspection and repair/replacement, alongside asymmetric rules allowing incremental repairs in non-POS cases but not POS cases (p. 78). While the large unadjusted compliance gap (63.86% POS vs. 19.85% non-POS within 120 days; crude OR = 7.225; adjusted OR = 4.27) reflects real transactional leverage during property sales, causal attribution to the POS inspection rule itself carries serious risk of bias.

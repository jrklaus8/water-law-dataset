## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Some concern (JBI Cohort Studies narrative judgment: 7 Yes, 4 Unclear, 0 No). Dos Santos and LeGrand (2013) analyze retrospective life-history cohorts reconstructed from Burkina Faso's nationally representative 2000 EMIUB survey (2,839 Ouagadougou respondents plus 294 former residents interviewed elsewhere in the country) using Kaplan-Meier curves and time-varying Cox proportional hazards models to estimate time to first access (n=2,134) and first loss (n=1,226 in Table 1; n=1,220 in text) of on-plot piped water. Methodological strengths include probability sampling with national capture of internal out-migrants from Ouagadougou, strict outcome-free baseline risk sets with delayed entry, structured AGEVEN age-event calendars to aid temporal ordering, and explicit modeling of time-varying housing tenure interacted with municipal zoning status (downtown, zoned periphery, non-zoned periphery). Key limitations driving 'Some concern' are multi-decade retrospective recall vulnerability for both exposures and water-transition dates without ONEA utility verification, survivor and international-emigrant selection bias inherent to interviewing living residents in 2000, unmeasured time-varying household income/wealth and connection-cost confounders, pooling of exclusive private taps with shared/potentially locked compound taps, and minor statistical reporting omissions (no confidence intervals/standard errors, no proportional-hazards diagnostics, and a 1,226 vs 1,220 sample-size discrepancy).

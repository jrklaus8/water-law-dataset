## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This primary qualitative study (semi-structured interviews and ethnographic site visits with 32 women and local water source managers in Gressier, Haiti) demonstrates moderate methodological concern on the CASP Qualitative Checklist (5 'Yes', 1 'No', and 3 'Can't tell' ratings across Q1-Q9, plus high substantive value on Q10). Strengths include clearly articulated aims (Q1), an appropriate qualitative/ethnographic design incorporating water-source site visits and iterative probing to saturation (Q2, Q3, Q5), and rich, nuanced reporting of thematic findings that contrast community member and source-manager perspectives (Q9). Key methodological limitations include the absence of researcher reflexivity regarding foreign academic positionality and local community-translator dynamics (Q6: No); incomplete reporting of recruitment for the parent 304-woman survey, unexplained non-participation by 34 of 66 consensus-screened women, undocumented recruitment of source managers, and potential conformity bias from restricting selection to top-10% cultural-consensus peers (Q4: Can't tell); lack of reported IRB/ethics committee approval or minor-protection protocols despite enrolling participants over age 16 (Q7: Can't tell); and minimal description of the NVivo coding and thematic derivation process (Q8: Can't tell).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Study S1036 (Francois, Kakeu, and Kouame 2021) is an observational longitudinal country-year dynamic panel econometric analysis of 44 Sub-Saharan African countries over 2002-2015 using two-step system GMM (Blundell and Bond 1998) to estimate the effect of institutional governance quality (six Worldwide Governance Indicators) on national, rural, and urban access to improved sanitation. Appraised under ROBINS-I, the study is judged at Serious overall risk of bias, driven primarily by Domain 1 (confounding) and Domain 3 (classification/measurement of institutional exposures). Specifically, baseline models treat institutional quality as strictly exogenous and omit time fixed effects; several extended specifications exhibit explosive lagged dependent variable coefficients (>1.0); and specifications instrumenting endogenous institutions with internal lags exceed the safe instrument-to-country ratio (r < 1), weakening overidentification tests without any external quasi-experimental source of variation. Furthermore, institutional exposures are measured exclusively through subjective WGI perception composites rather than formal sanitation laws or de jure regulatory institutions, which—as the authors themselves acknowledge—are susceptible to simultaneity with broader economic conditions and cannot establish causality or isolate specific legal mechanisms. Selection into the unbalanced 44-country panel, MDG-era co-interventions, missing country-year observations (~12%-17%), and smoothed aggregate sanitation series pose Moderate concerns, while no pre-analysis plan is reported (No information for D7).

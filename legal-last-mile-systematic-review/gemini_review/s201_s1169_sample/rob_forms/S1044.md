## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Gonzalez-Gomez, Garcia-Rubio, and Gonzalez-Martinez (2014) present a doctrinal-empirical institutional case study and policy analysis of urban water management in Spain. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment; no numeric score computed), the paper demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity (D2), exposure definition across public and PPP management modalities (D3), and institutional-contextual analysis of municipal fragmentation, concession incentives, oligopolistic market structure, and constitutional limits on national regulation (D10), alongside adequate legal source accuracy (D1) and descriptive outcome definition (D4). However, empirical rigor is substantially constrained by the absence of a dedicated Methods section: primary fieldwork claiming census coverage of Spanish municipalities (23% of municipalities and 55% of population under private management; 67% municipal / 75% population private share held by AGBAR and Aqualia), bidder counts from 37 tender processes in 2013-2014, and observations from tariff revision proceedings lack sampling documentation (D5), systematic case selection (D6), measurement and coding protocols (D7, D12), dataset replicability (D11), and researcher reflexivity (D13). Causal claims and performance comparisons rely on unadjusted descriptive statistics and a non-systematic narrative review of prior Spanish studies without formal causal identification (D8) or empirical confounding control (D9), although the authors candidly acknowledge industry opacity, missing official tap-water quality notifications (58% of population missing in SINAC in 2011), and selection of private operators into larger, more profitable municipalities.

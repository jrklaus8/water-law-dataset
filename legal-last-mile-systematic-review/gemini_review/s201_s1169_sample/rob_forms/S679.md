## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Goldin (2010) presents a primary qualitative field and documentary case study of participatory water governance in the Breede-Overberg Water Management Area (Western Cape, South Africa) under the National Water Act (Act 36 of 1998) and Water Services Act (Act 108 of 1997). Drawing on contrasting local histories (Ruensveld/Duivenhoks commercial farming water schemes vs. the coloured fishing village of Kassiesbaai), primary interviews (2001-2004), and observations of Catchment Management Agency (CMA) public meetings and informal corridor ('stoep') spaces, the paper clearly articulates its aims (Q1: Yes), uses an appropriate qualitative approach (Q2: Yes), and states coherent thematic findings on how technocratic IWRM discourse, entrenched elite networks, and shame obstruct inclusive reform (Q9: Yes). However, appraised against the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits high methodological concern (4 'Can't tell' and 2 'No' ratings across the 9 scored items): it contains no dedicated Methods section, leaving case/meeting selection rationale (Q3: Can't tell), participant recruitment (Q4: Can't tell), data collection procedures and sample size (Q5: Can't tell), and research ethics/consent (Q7: Can't tell) unreported, while omitting researcher reflexivity (Q6: No) and rigorous, transparent qualitative coding/analysis (Q8: No).

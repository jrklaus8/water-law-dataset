## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High methodological concern under the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies. Strengths of Ko (2024) include complete administrative coverage of all 152 city and county (si/gun) local water supply utilities in South Korea across three time points (2010, 2016, and 2021), reliance on standardized official national datasets (Ministry of Environment Water Supply Statistics, MOIS, NEC, and Statistics Korea), transparent reporting of OLS robustness checks alongside Tobit estimates, and candid acknowledgment of analytical limitations in Footnote 14 and Section 4.2. However, severe construct validity and econometric limitations compromise the empirical findings. Most critically, four of the six service outcomes (average unit price, cost recovery rate, revenue water ratio, and water supply rate) are intensive ratios or unit prices rather than extensive divisible totals; inserting them into Coulter's population-share inequity formula (Xi/S - Ei) treats municipal water rates or unit prices proportional to population share as the benchmark of equity, yielding implausible theoretical unit prices (e.g., 5,734 won/m3 for a populous city) and causing municipal inequity scores to be mechanically dominated by population share Ei. Similarly, operationalizing customer satisfaction via raw complaint counts treats lower-than-expected complaints per capita as inequitable. In the second-stage regressions, estimating separate cross-sectional models for each year without panel fixed effects, clustered standard errors, or controls for population size/density and terrain leaves estimates heavily confounded by municipal scale—reflected in nearly identical regression coefficients across substantively distinct water indicators in Tables 9-13—and Tables 12 and 13 exhibit duplicated model-fit statistics.

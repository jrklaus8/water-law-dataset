## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Inha and Hukka (2019) present a single-city qualitative case study of Seattle's water supply, wastewater, and stormwater services based on a narrative policy/literature review and individual semi-structured interviews with 12 local water professionals (with 6 to 8 respondents answering per subsector). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist (the structured rank-order tallying across 6-8 respondents per subsector functions as an interview elicitation aid rather than a standalone quantitative survey component warranting MMAT), the study clearly states its aims (Q1: Yes), adopts an appropriate qualitative case-study approach (Q2: Yes; Q3: Yes), and presents well-structured subsector findings (Q9: Yes). However, substantial methodological concerns arise from unreported recruitment and participant institutional composition (Q4: Can't tell), omitted interview recording/transcription and setting details (Q5: Can't tell), lack of researcher reflexivity despite researcher curation of the candidate policy lists (Q6: No), absent ethics/consent reporting (Q7: Can't tell), and opaque coding verification alongside unadjusted pooling of incomplete and tied rankings with zero verbatim participant quotes (Q8: Can't tell). Moreover, both legal exposure importance and service resilience are assessed purely via interviewee perceptions rather than objective empirical outcome data.

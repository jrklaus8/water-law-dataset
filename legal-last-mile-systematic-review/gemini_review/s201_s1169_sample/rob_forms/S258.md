## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This primary qualitative participatory study used photovoice, Chichewa-language gallery walks and PHOTO group discussions, phenomenological coding in NVivo 12, and community validation exhibitions to examine postpartum women's lived experiences of water scarcity in two rural catchment areas of Thyolo District, Malawi (n = 8 female co-researchers: 4 mothers and 4 guardians). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study raises low-to-moderate methodological concern overall (9 'Yes' and 0 'Can't tell' or 'No' ratings across Q1-Q9, plus clear practical value under Q10). Strengths include transparent alignment between research aims and participatory visual methodology, explicit researcher positionality and anti-colonial partnership framing, clear ethical safeguards for visual data, participant-led selection and captioning of 44 photographs from 281 taken, voices represented across all eight co-researchers, and post-analysis community exhibitions for validation. Key methodological limitations are the small purposive sample recruited via health facilities in high-vulnerability sites, lack of explicit detail on audio-recording and verbatim transcription/translation verification of Chichewa group discussions, potential social desirability or authority bias from the unplanned workshop attendance of three local leaders, and reliance on self-reported perceptions rather than clinical or microbiological measures for health and water-quality outcomes (with formal water law/governance addressed only as background context rather than empirically measured).

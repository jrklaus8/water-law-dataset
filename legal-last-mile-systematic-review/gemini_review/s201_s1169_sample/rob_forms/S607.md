## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Romano, Nelson-Nuñez, and LaVanchy (2021) present a comparative institutional and legal-historical three-country case study examining the evolution of state legal frameworks, decentralization policies, and regulatory relationships with community-based water management (CBWM) organizations in rural Nicaragua, Honduras, and Costa Rica from the 1970s through 2020. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured, non-validated qualitative judgment rather than a numeric score), the study demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity (D2) and rich institutional contextualization (D10) of central agencies, municipal decentralization, donor/NGO coproduction, grassroots federation networks (REDCAPS, AHJASA, CLOCSAS), and practical barriers to formal CBWM registration. Legal source accuracy (D1), institutional exposure conceptualization (D3), and macro-level country selection rationale (D5) are adequate, though statutory descriptions rely on secondary literature rather than primary legal texts. Methodological rigor is weak with respect to empirical selection procedures (D6), outcome operationalization (D4), measurement transparency across heterogeneous and inconsistent secondary statistics (D7), causal identification (D8), control of historical/political confounders (D9), replication and coding transparency in the absence of a formal Methods section (D11, D12), and researcher reflexivity (D13). Accordingly, the paper provides valuable institutional-historical synthesis and policy hypothesis generation, but weak internal validity for causal claims linking specific statutory reforms to rural water service outcomes.

| `selection_bias` | High concern: Pathumthani was purposively chosen by the Thai government as the first water privatisation site due to its financial attractiveness and proximity to Bangkok (PDF pp. 8, 24 n. 4). | `confounding` | High concern: No unprivatised comparison municipality or control group exists, and no multivariable regression or quasi-experimental adjustment was performed to separate the 1998 privatisation from 1998–2003 post-Asian-financial-crisis macroeconomic recovery, municipal groundwater/land-subsidence policies, or household-level covariates (PDF pp. 8, 14–15). |
| `attrition` | High concern (differential post-survey exclusion): Of 288 targeted households, 274 questionnaires were completed (all 14 non-respondents were in formal communities), and 62 of 274 completed questionnaires (22.6%) were excluded from analysis—predominantly due to the requirement of continuous residence since 1998—with severe differential exclusion in informal settlements (52/138 [37.7%] dropped) versus formal settlements (10/136 [7.4%] dropped) (PDF pp. 11–13, Table 3). |

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High concern. Zaki and Amin (2009) conducted a single-site 2003 stratified cross-sectional household survey (288 targeted, 274 completed, 212 analysed) in Amphoe Muang, Pathumthani province, Thailand, asking households to recall their pre-privatisation (1998) water supply conditions alongside current (2003) conditions and comparing informal versus formal settlements and income-poor versus non-poor subgroups, supplemented by aggregate Provincial Waterworks Authority (PWA) operational and water-quality records. While the paper provides valuable institutional documentation of Thailand's unbundled BOOT concession structure (where PWA retained retail tariff setting and billing) and verified 2003 household connections and monthly bills directly, it has major methodological limitations for causal or comparative inference: (1) there is no unprivatised comparison municipality to separate the 1998 concession from post-1997 Asian financial crisis recovery and secular urban infrastructure growth; (2) all 1998 household baseline measures relied on 5-year retrospective recall in 2003 and subjective binary perceptions of water/service quality; (3) restricting the analytic sample to households continuously resident since 1998 excluded mobile slum residents and dropped 37.7% (52/138) of interviewed informal-settlement households versus 7.4% (10/136) in formal settlements; (4) household legal tenure was proxied ecologically at the community level rather than measured directly, and 2003 income was applied retroactively to 1998; and (5) statistical testing relied on unadjusted, unpaired, unclustered chi-squared tests across bivariate contingency tables.

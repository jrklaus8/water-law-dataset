## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

S535 is a primary qualitative multi-country study examining how national and local regulators govern urban road-transported faecal sludge emptying and transport in five East and Southern African countries (Kenya, Mozambique, Tanzania, Uganda and Zambia). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study demonstrates low-to-moderate methodological concern overall (8 'Yes' items, 1 'Can't tell' item on Q8 data analysis rigor, and strong conceptual value on Q10). Strengths include clear research aims, transparent purposive selection of 'positive outlier' regulatory systems, deductive-inductive coding grounded in literature and secondary qualitative data, formal ethics approval (CURES/23444/2024), respondent validation (>70% informant confirmation), open archiving of anonymized transcripts, and candid reflexivity regarding Western institutional positionality and social desirability bias. Key limitations include single-author formal coding without reported double-coding, filtering of Results to consensus themes across sites, absence of participant IDs on quotes (with zero local regulator interviews in Mozambique), and reliance on senior regulator self-reports without triangulation from informal manual emptiers, competing municipal enforcement agencies or household sanitation outcomes.

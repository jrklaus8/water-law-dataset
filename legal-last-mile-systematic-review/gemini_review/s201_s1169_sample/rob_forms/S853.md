## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Gomez, Perdiguero, and Sanz (2019) conduct an observational macro-panel regression analyzing national socioeconomic and governance correlates of rural drinking-water coverage (total improved, piped on premises, and other improved sources) across 135 low- and middle-income countries over a nominal 1990-2015 period using two-way (country and year) fixed-effects Prais-Winsten GLS models with heteroskedastic and panel-specific AR(1) errors. Methodological strengths include disaggregating WHO/UNICEF JMP rural water coverage into piped on premises versus other improved sources, stratifying analyses by World Bank income tiers, and accounting for time-invariant country heterogeneity, common year shocks, and serial correlation. However, overall methodological concern is High (Q1, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7 = Yes; Q2, Q3 = Unclear; Q8 = No). First, all six Worldwide Governance Indicators (including Rule of Law and Regulatory Quality) are collapsed into three dummy categories ('Very weak', 'Weak', 'Good') in Figure 2, but all numerical cutoff values are omitted from Figure 2 and the text, rendering the primary institutional exposures unverifiable. Second, unaddressed missing data reduces the effective sample from 3,510 potential country-years (135 countries x 26 years) to N = 1,087-1,713 in global models and N = 1,021 total across the three income-group models (~71% missingness), with no missing-data diagnostics or outcome summary statistics reported. Third, global regressions enter LogGNI, female primary completion, and agriculture into separate equations due to multicollinearity (inducing severe omitted-variable bias), while subgroup regressions selectively drop governance variables via preliminary Wald tests and stratify 1990-2015 trajectories on end-of-period (2015) GNI per capita. Finally, several reporting errors appear (claiming statistical significance on p. 15 for an insignificant Table 5 piped-water rural growth coefficient of 0.267 [SE 0.236], mislabeling Wald p-values as standard errors in Tables A4-A6, and misaligning rows in Tables 6-7).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Appelblad Fredby and Nilsson (2013) present a historical-geographical qualitative case study of urban water provision in Kampala, combining colonial archival records (UK and Kenya National Archives), NWSC and Ugandan ministry reports, and primary field interviews conducted in 2006 and 2008 in Kisenyi I and II parishes with 30 standpost vendors, utility managers/staff, consultants, NGO representatives, and community leaders. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study raises high methodological concern (3 Yes, 4 Can't tell, and 2 No ratings across Q1-Q9). Its strengths lie in clearly stated research aims (Q1), an appropriate qualitative archival-and-fieldwork approach (Q2), well-documented historical and institutional tracing of utility reforms, and a coherent synthesis of findings linking colonial spatial dualism and insecure tenure to the failure of private-connection subsidies (Q9). However, the article has no dedicated Methods section in the main body, relegating its primary fieldwork description to Endnote 61. Consequently, case selection and research design justification (Q3), informant recruitment and non-inclusion of low-income end-user tenants (Q4), interview and recording/translation procedures (Q5), and research ethics/consent safeguards (Q7) cannot be evaluated ('Can't tell'), while researcher reflexivity regarding NWSC/Kampala Water gatekeeping (Q6) and auditable qualitative coding or verbatim evidentiary support for key field claims (Q8) are absent ('No').

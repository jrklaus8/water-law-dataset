## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Guardia, Rossello, and Garriga (2014) present a single-city archival institutional and urban-historical case study of Barcelona's century-long transition to a modern domestic water and sanitation system (1867–1967). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment; no numeric score), the study demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity and rich institutional contextualization—tracing municipal fiscal limits, private utility consolidation under SGAB, sanitary ordinances (1891), failed municipalization bids, landlord trickle-feed contracts undermining the 1925 Royal Decree on water metering, and post-1950 surface-water catchments from the Llobregat and Ter rivers—supported by traceable archival call numbers (AMCB, AHCB, Fundacion AGBAR Fonds). However, as a traditional humanities narrative with no Methods section, it exhibits weak sampling, selection, and measurement transparency (relying on purposively chosen dossiers and unverified corporate/municipal flow statistics), lacks formal source-bias reflexivity toward private utility records, and supports causal claims solely through chronological narrative rather than counterfactual identification.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This large-scale cluster-randomized controlled trial (S366; Quattrochi et al., 2021) evaluated the Democratic Republic of Congo's national Healthy Villages and Schools (Villages et Ecoles Assainis, VEA) WASH and local governance programme across 121 clusters (328 analyzed rural villages; 1,312 households) in four conflict-affected provinces. Appraised with the RoB 2 cluster-randomized trials variant, the trial exhibits strong internal validity in cluster allocation, retention, and estimation: clusters were grouped spatially to limit spillovers, randomized simultaneously via stratified computer allocation with good balance on time-invariant household traits (D1a: Low), analyzed by intention-to-treat with cluster-robust standard errors (D2: Low), and achieved 100% cluster retention across all 121 clusters and 328 villages (D3: Low). However, overall risk of bias is High under the RoB 2 algorithm primarily due to Domain 4 (High risk): every primary and secondary outcome—including improved water and sanitation use, water collection time/volume, hygiene/sanitation behaviours, water satisfaction, and village water governance—was self-reported at endline by unblinded participants following an intensive community behaviour-change and 'Healthy Village' certification campaign, without independent observational spot-checks or water-quality testing, and with a programme-participation survey module that could unmask enumerators. Additional Some concerns arise in Domain 1b (endline random-walk household recruitment without a baseline roster or reported refusal rates) and Domain 5 (disclosed post-hoc splitting of the secondary health-behaviour index after viewing the data).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Structured non-validated appraisal under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework indicates moderate-to-high methodological concern for inferential claims, alongside strong institutional contextualization. Substantively, the study conducts a single-city urban water, waste, and climate governance diagnostic of Bandung, Indonesia, applying the City Blueprint® Approach: secondary national and municipal indicator benchmarking via the Trends and Pressures Framework (24 indicators) and City Blueprint Performance Framework (24 indicators; BCI = 2.6), paired with a semiquantitative Governance Capacity Framework matrix (27 indicators scored ++ to -- across 5 water challenges in Table 1). Its chief strengths lie in rich local institutional context (D10: strong) and clear statutory/jurisdictional framing and indicator architecture (D1–D4, D7: adequate), accurately situating PDAM Tirtawening, Bojongsoang WWTP, community-based sanitation, waste banks, slum legalization, and Spatial Planning Act No. 26/2007 compliance deficits. However, primary methodological transparency for the core GCF component is weak: although the Results embed coded stakeholder references (e.g., WW01–WW04, SW01–SW04, FL02–FL03, UH01–UH03, WS02), the Methods section omits any description of stakeholder sampling, recruitment, interviewee counts, coding rubrics, or inter-rater verification (D5, D6, D11, D12: weak), and provides no researcher reflexivity despite co-authors evaluating their own proprietary framework (D13: weak). Finally, as a single-city cross-sectional baseline using mixed-vintage secondary statistics (2006–2020), causal identification and confounding control for cross-city and governance-to-outcome claims are weak (D8, D9: weak).

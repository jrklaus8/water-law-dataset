## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Majuru, Suhrcke, and Hunter (2016) is a systematic review with thematic narrative synthesis (no meta-analysis) of 28 empirical cross-sectional studies examining household coping strategies, coping costs, socio-economic distribution, and effectiveness in response to unreliable domestic water supplies in developing countries. Appraised with AMSTAR 2, overall confidence in the review's results is Critically Low due to multiple flaws in critical domains: no a priori review protocol or registration was stated (Q2); full-text eligibility was restricted to English without methodological justification and search dates were omitted (Q4); the 13 full-text articles excluded after screening were not listed with individual reasons (Q7); individual study quality was appraised by summing arbitrary points across six broad reporting/validity items into a composite 0-12 score rather than evaluating domain-specific confounding, selection, and measurement biases appropriate to observational designs (Q9); and findings from studies rated High risk of bias were synthesized uncritically alongside Low-risk studies without sensitivity discussion (Q13). Additional non-critical weaknesses include lack of duplicate study selection (Q5) and duplicate data extraction (Q6), lack of justification for study design inclusion (Q3), and non-reporting of primary study funding sources (Q10). Key strengths are well-defined review questions and eligibility criteria (Q1), multi-database searching including grey literature and reference lists, structured tabular description of included studies and 15 coping strategies (Q8), thoughtful explanation of heterogeneity in coping costs and willingness-to-pay estimates across settings (Q14), and an explicit conflict-of-interest declaration (Q16).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Amorim et al. (2025) evaluate whether regulatory formalization and enforcement inspections by Minas Gerais's state water regulator (Arsae-MG) increased municipal implementation of the water and sanitation social tariff by the state utility Copasa-MG (and subsidiary Copanor). Using an unprecedented balanced municipal panel (N = 572 municipalities in 2020-2021; N = 570 in a 2018-2021 robustness check), the authors apply paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and random-effects GLS regressions on the Degree of Implementation of the Social Tariff (DIST) Index. Appraised under ROBINS-I, overall risk of bias is Serious, driven by Domain 1 (Confounding): because all Copasa-MG municipalities received the regulatory and enforcement package simultaneously and no unexposed comparator utility/state data were available, the treatment variable (REGU = 1 in 2021, 0 otherwise) is collinear with time. While the 2018-2021 robustness check demonstrates that 2021 implementation (87.53%) rose well above pre-pandemic 2018 (76.1%) and 2019 (78.3%) levels—showing the gain was not merely recovery from the 2020 pandemic dip (68.28%)—the design cannot disentangle Arsae-MG's August 2020 inspection and April 2021 Regulation No. 150 from concurrent Minas Gerais State Law No. 23,670 (July 2020) or broader temporal shocks, and random-effects estimation is not tested against municipal fixed effects. Selection bias and missing-data bias are Low given near-census administrative coverage, while intervention classification and outcome measurement carry Moderate risk because REGU bundles multiple legal/enforcement events and the aggregate DIST ratio is right-censored at 100% without household-level identity verification between utility billing records and CadÚnico.

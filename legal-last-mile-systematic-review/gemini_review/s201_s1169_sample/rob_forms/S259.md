## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Geremew et al. (2024) conducted a convergent mixed-methods cross-sectional assessment of 10 small-town piped water utilities in Eastern Ethiopia (Oromia and Somali regions) across August and December 2022, applying and adapting the six-domain 'How tough is WASH' climate-resilience framework. Per MMAT (2018) guidance, no numeric composite score is calculated, and overall methodological quality is bounded by the weakest empirical components (Moderate-to-high methodological concern). Strengths include clear research objectives (S1, S2), multi-modal integration across WHO sanitary inspections, membrane-filtration microbial testing, DPD residual chlorine testing, ArcGIS DEM flood-risk mapping, household surveys, and semi-structured stakeholder interviews (5.1-5.3), and explicit reconciliation of divergences between household self-reports and utility manager interviews regarding bill payment and water treatment (5.4). However, substantial limitations affect both empirical strands (5.5 = No): towns were purposively sampled and household random sampling was restricted to utility piped-water customer lists—excluding up to 80% of residents in low-coverage towns like Aweday (4.2 = No); actual household response rates, achieved sample sizes across the two seasonal rounds, socio-demographic characteristics, and tabular survey denominators/confidence intervals are omitted (4.4 = Can't tell; 4.5 = Can't tell; plus an unexplained missing microbial value for Babile in Table 2); and qualitative findings are presented solely as narrative summaries without a single verbatim quotation, coding audit detail, or reflexivity regarding consenting participants in the presence of a local water utility expert (1.3 = Can't tell; 1.4 = No).

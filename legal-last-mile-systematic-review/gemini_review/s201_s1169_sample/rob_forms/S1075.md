## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High methodological concern. Silvestre (2012) conducts a single-year (2009) analytical cross-sectional study of all 308 Portuguese municipalities using regulatory data from the Portuguese Water Sector Regulator (ERSAR) to compare water tariffs, customer complaints, and drinking-water quality compliance across public vs. private property, five institutional management models, and four organizational cost categories. Key strengths include census-level national municipal coverage and standardized regulatory metrics for domestic water tariffs (at 60, 120, and 180 m3) and EU Water Framework Directive parametric testing/compliance. However, internal validity for comparing institutional models is severely compromised by (1) complete absence of multivariable adjustment or confounding control (e.g., municipal population size, urban/rural density, baseline network dilapidation, and fiscal stress driving selection into PPPs); (2) systematic cost-accounting asymmetry prior to Law 194/2009, whereby municipal bureaucracies shared unrecorded overhead with town halls while PPPs and municipal corporations internalized full capital and operating costs; (3) discretization of organizational costs into four categories without disclosed Euro cut-offs and use of raw, unnormalized complaint counts that scale with customer population and tariff dissatisfaction; and (4) inappropriate statistical methods, including separate univariate one-way ANOVAs without post-hoc tests and parametric linearity/correlation analyses applied to unordered nominal management-model codes (1-5). Consequently, the study's strong causal policy conclusion recommending a halt to water PPPs is not supported by its cross-sectional bivariate design.

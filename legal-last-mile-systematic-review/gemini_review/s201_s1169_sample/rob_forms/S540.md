## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This primary qualitative case study investigates Morocco's national water policy (1995-2020) and local water governance challenges in the Middle Draa Valley using MAXQDA-assisted deductive/inductive content analysis of 10 policy documents, 37 semi-structured interviews (31 respondents across government agencies, irrigation water user associations, and inhabitants), and 1 focus group with 5 female cooperative members. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study demonstrates clear aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative design combining documentary and field inquiry (Q2-Q3: Yes), purposeful and maximal-variation/snowball sampling (Q4: Yes), transparent interview guides and deductive coding rules (Q5, Q8: Yes), and well-structured, policy-relevant findings (Q9: Yes; Q10: Valuable). Methodological concerns are moderate overall (1 No, 1 Can't tell): researcher reflexivity and positionality are not examined despite cross-lingual interviews covering illegal well digging and bribery (Q6: No); ethical reporting is limited to participant anonymization codes with no statement on informed consent or ethics/IRB review (Q7: Can't tell); Mezguita oasis was omitted without explanation and individual interviews were heavily male-skewed (26 of 31); several national policy plans were not publicly accessible and were analyzed via secondary official presentations/websites; and water/salinity/depletion outcomes rely on qualitative perceptions and secondary studies rather than direct physical measurements.

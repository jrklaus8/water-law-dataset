## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Schuller and Levey (2014) present a primary mixed-methods investigation of WASH and clinic service gaps in Port-au-Prince metropolitan IDP camps following the January 2010 Haiti earthquake and October 2010 cholera outbreak. Appraised with the MMAT (2018) using the Quantitative non-randomized (analytic cross-sectional) block (3.1–3.5), the study's main strengths are its probability sampling frame drawn systematically from the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (Annexe 2), direct researcher field observation and duplicate quality-control revisits for physical toilet counts and water availability (p. S4), trilingual semi-structured interviews with 57 humanitarian and government officials alongside camp-level testimonies (p. S3), and strong substantive integration of quantitative patterns with qualitative institutional explanations (5.1–5.4 all 'Yes'). However, per MMAT 2018 guidance that overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component, overall methodological concern is moderate-to-high: verified water outcome data were missing for 28.3% of analysed camps (71/99; item 3.3 'No'), statistical analyses relied on unadjusted two-way and selected three-way chi-square crosstabs without multivariable confounding control or socioeconomic covariates despite strong non-random selection of NGOs into central and public-land camps (item 3.4 'No'), pre-survey closure of unmanaged camps inflated the sample share of NGO-managed camps (33% vs. 20.8%–22% in DTM), minor statistical/sample reporting inconsistencies appear in the text (pp. S1, S3–S4, S9, S11), and qualitative coding and researcher reflexivity (including author advocacy in a studied camp; p. S7) are minimally detailed (item 1.3 'Can't tell'; item 5.5 'No').

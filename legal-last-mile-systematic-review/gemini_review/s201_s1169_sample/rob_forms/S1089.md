## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High methodological concern (all 8 JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional items rated 'No'). S1089 combines a narrative institutional overview of decentralization in Pakistan (18th Constitutional Amendment, Punjab and KPK Local Government Acts, Katchi Abadis Act) and five brief secondary 'good practice' vignettes with primary empirical data from a cross-sectional household survey conducted by the Urban Unit in two low-income settlements: Kotli Ghazi in Lahore (Punjab) and Ganj Bazar in Peshawar (KPK). Methodological reporting and internal validity are severely limited. The chapter omits survey dates, sampling frames, household recruitment criteria, response rates, questionnaire wording, and even the sample size in prose (total N = 80 is only deducible from row sums in Tables 1 and 2, where one row anomalously sums to 82 and several percentages are miscalculated). Respondents were predominantly male household heads, limiting representativeness for marginalized women discussed in the chapter. Water and sanitation outcomes ('safe drinking water' 52%, 'community level sanitation and waste disposal' 42%, 'restrooms' 42%) rely on undefined subjective self-reports, and the cross-province comparison attributing higher service access in Lahore (62.5-87.5%) than Peshawar (22.5-65.0%) to local governance arrangements is completely unadjusted for glaring neighbourhood-level confounders such as refugee/IDP concentration and peri-urban versus inner-city settlement structure.

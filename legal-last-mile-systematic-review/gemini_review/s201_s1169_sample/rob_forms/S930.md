## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Diaz-Cayeros, Magaloni, and Ruiz-Euler (2014) conduct a comparative observational policy evaluation of Oaxaca's 1995 constitutional reform recognizing indigenous customary municipal governance (usos y costumbres) versus political party governance across 570 municipalities (564 on common support), combining kernel propensity score matching with first-differences across 1990–2010 census/conteo waves. Under ROBINS-I, the overall risk of bias is Serious, driven primarily by Domain 1 (Confounding): although first-differences remove time-invariant municipal unobservables, the propensity score conditions on multiple post-1995 covariates measured after the 1995 reform (including 2000 census GDP residuals, 2000 locality concentration, 1990–2010 indigenous-language share, and contemporaneous spatial lag of neighboring usos status), omits pre-reform public-good levels and pre-1995 trend checks, suffers from severe support thinness in the highest propensity block (212 treated municipalities matched against just 4 controls in Block 7), and faces plausible time-varying confounding from post-1997 poverty-formula federal infrastructure transfers (FAIS/FISM) and baseline catch-up effects. Moderate concerns also arise in selection/sample reporting (retroactive use of the 2000 usos roster and an unexplained 412 treated / 228 control count in Table 5), intervention classification (pre-1995 de facto customary governance and 1995–2000 switchers, plus inclusion of pre-reform years in 1990–2000 differences), missing-data reporting in auxiliary surveys, and selective narrative emphasis across multiple overlapping periods and level vs. percentage-change metrics in the absence of a pre-analysis plan (notably, water coverage ATTs are uniformly null across all periods, and 1990–2010 sewerage ATTs are also statistically insignificant despite positive 1995–2005 and 2000–2010 rate-of-change estimates). Primary infrastructure outcome measurement from standardized INEGI censuses carries Low risk of differential measurement bias.

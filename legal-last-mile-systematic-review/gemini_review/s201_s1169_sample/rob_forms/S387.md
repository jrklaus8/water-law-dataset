## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Glavanits and Fenyes (2026) present an exploratory convergent mixed-methods study embedded within a feminist legal and regulatory analysis of public toilet access in Hungary. Appraised under the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018, combining Qualitative items 1.1-1.5, Quantitative Descriptive items 4.1-4.5, and Mixed Methods items 5.1-5.5), the study demonstrates high methodological concern (weak overall empirical quality, as overall quality in MMAT cannot exceed its weakest component). Strengths include clear exploratory hypotheses (S1-S2), a sound rationale for triangulating self-reported perceptions, open-ended user accounts, and chronometric restroom observations (5.1-5.2), verbatim thematic illustrations (1.4), thoughtful attention to cross-strand nuances regarding unisex acceptability and hygiene (5.4), and accurate doctrinal synthesis of EU/UN equality norms and comparative building codes (Table 1). However, serious empirical limitations affect the quantitative and integrative strands: (1) convenience student and social-media sampling yields a small, non-representative survey sample (N = 97; 72.2% female, 63.9% aged 18-29; response rate unreported; 7 cases omitted in regressions alongside an unexplained 'Hungarian subsample' note) and a single 40-minute campus restroom observation window (N = 56) (4.1, 4.2, 4.4); (2) survey reliability is below conventional thresholds (Cronbach's alpha = 0.601) and Table 2's footnote directly contradicts the main text regarding Likert coding direction, while chronometric timing conflates internal waiting with fixture use and shows conflicting summary means between Table 4 and the text (4.3); (3) Hypothesis H4 is evaluated using raw means without variance or inferential tests, and item-level survey reporting omits non-significant items (4.5); and (4) Sections 4.1 and 4.5 repeatedly invoke 'queuing simulations' as objective modeling evidence despite no simulation methods or results appearing anywhere in the article (5.3, 5.5).

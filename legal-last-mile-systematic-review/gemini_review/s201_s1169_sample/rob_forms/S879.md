## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This study reports two sequential field experiments conducted with Nairobi's municipal water and sanitation utility (NCWSC) across informal settlements to improve service bill and last-mile connection loan repayment: (1) an individually randomized trial of face-to-face tenant engagement across 2,584 compounds with tenants, and (2) a two-stage street-level cluster-randomized controlled trial of transparent, credible service-contract disconnection notices across 147 street clusters (327 treated disconnection-eligible compounds, 322 within-cluster spillover controls, and 674 disconnection-eligible controls in 74 pure control clusters). Appraised using the RoB 2 cluster-randomized trial variant, the study has Low risk of bias for its primary payment and loan-repayment outcomes—which rely on centralized randomization, verified 4-year pre-intervention baseline balance, intention-to-treat estimation, explicit spillover testing, and 100% follow-up via automated Jisomee Mita utility billing records—and Some concerns overall when incorporating the 9-month endline owner and tenant survey outcomes (water/sanitation access, water expenditure, perceptions, well-being, and rental markets). Those concerns stem from sampling one tenant per compound at endline after treatment increased rental occupancy by 13.5 percentage points (D1b), unanalyzed item-level survey and meter-reading nonresponse (D3), unblinded respondent self-reports on secondary outcomes (D4), and the disclosed post-collection omission of under-five child health outcomes alongside restricting the endline survey sample to enforcement-treatment clusters rather than pure control clusters (D5).

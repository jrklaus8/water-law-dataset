## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High methodological concern under the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies (2 'Yes' items on sample inclusion criteria and setting/subject description; 6 'No' items on exposure validity, objective condition measurement, confounder identification, confounder adjustment, outcome validity, and statistical analysis). Although the study transparently describes its three Nepalese municipalities, randomly samples 103 of 138 WASH schemes constructed during 2011-2021, pilot-tested its instrument, and corroborates broad municipal warming and drying trends using 30-year DHM weather station records, its analytical claims rest on unadjusted bivariate linear regressions linking retrospective perceptions of climate and hazards to self-reported WASH system degradation from the same unblinded WUSC and caretaker respondents. No physical spring discharge or water-quality measurements were collected, confounding factors (such as physiographic zone, scheme age, road construction, and maintenance governance) were not controlled for, mediating pathways were mislabeled as moderation and analyzed piecemeal without accounting for municipal clustering, and purposive respondent eligibility rules produced a heavily male respondent pool (88% of WUSCs and 97% of caretakers).

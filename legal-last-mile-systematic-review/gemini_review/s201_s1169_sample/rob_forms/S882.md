## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Mason (2014) is a primary mixed-methods study investigating household financial, physical, and social resources and water security in Pinget, an urban barangay of Baguio City, Philippines, combining a stratified random household survey (N = 396; 95.2% response rate) with nested purposive in-depth interviews (n = 18). Appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018, using Qualitative 1.1–1.5, Quantitative non-randomized/analytical cross-sectional 3.1–3.5, and Mixed Methods 5.1–5.5), the study exhibits notable strengths in probability sampling within the target neighborhood, low missing data rigorously handled via multiple imputation, multivariable adjustment with compound-level clustering, and transparent mixed-methods integration—particularly in explaining divergences between null/counterintuitive survey coefficients and rich qualitative accounts of savings and neighbor water-sharing. Methodological concerns center on the quantitative component's cross-sectional observational design (precluding causal/temporal inference between household investments, water expenditures, and outcomes), field collection during an unusually wet dry season, reliance on unvalidated self-report and recall measures of water consumption and perceived cleanliness/ease without physical or microbiological verification, post-hoc dichotomization of two outcomes, and documented systematic misclassification in the hypothetical social borrowing network exposure. Per MMAT 2018 rules, no composite numeric score is calculated; overall methodological concern is judged as Moderate.

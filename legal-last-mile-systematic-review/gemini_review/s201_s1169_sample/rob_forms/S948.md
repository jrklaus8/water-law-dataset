## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Kujinga et al. (2013) conduct a convergent mixed-methods empirical study in Ngamiland District, Botswana, combining a structured survey of 554 households across 8 settlements (primary, tertiary, and ungazetted) with key informant interviews, 8 focus group discussions, informal interviews, participant observation, and review of water agency records. Appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018, using the Quantitative Descriptive block 4.1-4.5 alongside Qualitative 1.1-1.5 and Mixed-Methods 5.1-5.5), the study effectively integrates descriptive survey patterns with institutional and qualitative field evidence to show how official settlement gazettement rules, flooded boreholes, aging pipe networks, financial constraints, and fragmented DWA/NWDC mandates produce severe local water insecurity despite high national 'improved water source' coverage. However, per MMAT 2018 guidance that overall methodological quality cannot exceed the weakest component, the study carries moderate-to-high methodological concern. Key limitations include non-representative site selection (convenience sampling of villages, selection of Matlapana based on co-author residence, complete omission of Ngamiland's secondary settlement category, and purposive restriction of Maun to just two contrasting wards without sampling weights), complete non-reporting of household survey response rates, unadjusted descriptive statistical analysis without confounder control, a single-sentence description of qualitative thematic analysis without reflexivity, and unaddressed divergence between the 12-month survey availability measure in Figure 3 and textual claims of 100% water insecurity in river-dependent ungazetted settlements.

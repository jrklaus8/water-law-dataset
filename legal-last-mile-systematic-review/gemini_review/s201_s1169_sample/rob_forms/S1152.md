## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Marra (2008) is a desk-based single-country legal/policy institutional case study and feminist theoretical synthesis examining how Malawi's post-1995 water policy reforms (principally the 1995 Water Works Act and 1999 Water Resources Policy and Strategy, alongside the 1998 Integrated Trade and Industry Policy and 2000 National Gender Policy) affect rural and impoverished women. Assessed under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment, with no numeric score), the study exhibits weak empirical and doctrinal rigor overall (3 'adequate' domains and 10 'weak' domains). Its chief strengths lie in situating the reforms within Malawi's institutional and socio-legal context—highlighting Ministry capacity constraints, male leadership on female-staffed Community Water Boards, household cash-control asymmetries, and matrilineal versus patrilineal regional differences—and directly critiquing vague action verbs in the 2000 National Gender Policy. However, the paper contains no Methods section, no systematic search or document-selection protocol, and no primary empirical data. Crucially, the core 1995 Water Works Act, 1999 Water Resources Policy and Strategy, and Malawian Constitution are analyzed secondhand via secondary literature rather than primary statutory texts, empirical claims rely on secondary reports and journalistic/NGO web sources, and gendered impacts of the water reforms are explicitly framed largely as predicted consequences rather than measured post-reform outcomes.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Jiménez, Mtango, and Cairncross (2014) present a primary qualitative multi-tier governance and political economy case study of local government roles during the first year (2012-2013) of Tanzania's National Sanitation Campaign (NSC). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study demonstrates clear aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative PGPE framework and multi-level design (Q2: Yes; Q3: Yes), purposive multi-tier recruitment across 6 districts of varying initial performance totaling 81 interviews and group discussions with only one refusal (Q4: Yes), structured multi-source field data collection (Q5: Yes), clear synthesis of findings across five project management functions (Q9: Yes), and strong policy value (Q10). Methodological concerns center on three areas (1 No and 2 Can't tell items): complete absence of researcher reflexivity regarding author/NGO/donor positionality (Q6: No), partial ethical reporting that notes informed purpose, voluntary participation, and confidentiality without mentioning institutional ethics committee approval (Q7: Can't tell), and opaque qualitative data analysis procedures relying on unquoted field notes without documented coding, inter-district comparison, or verbatim participant quotations (Q8: Can't tell). Overall methodological concern is Moderate.

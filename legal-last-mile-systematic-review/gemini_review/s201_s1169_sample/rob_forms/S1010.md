## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Sarkar (2019) is a convergent mixed-methods case study combining descriptive analysis of Kenyan national/Nairobi regulatory water coverage statistics (WSRB reports), a structured cross-sectional survey of 258 standpipe-reliant households across all 13 villages in the Mathare informal settlement (2016-2017), and qualitative focus group discussions, key informant interviews, and field observations. Under MMAT (2018) criteria (where overall methodological quality cannot exceed the weakest component), the study demonstrates strong mixed-methods rationale and substantive triangulation—effectively contrasting official WSRB coverage gains and formal Water Act 2002 licensing/tariff rules against lived realities of overcrowded standpipes, 70-94% monopoly retail markups during supply shortages, ATM tank water theft, and tribal queuing favouritism—but exhibits high methodological concern due to critical weaknesses in both underlying empirical strands. Specifically, the quantitative descriptive strand provides no 2016-2017 sampling frame or random selection mechanism for informal-settlement households, omits response rates, imputes monthly kiosk revenues using an unrealistic 50 L/capita/day benchmark, measures willingness to pay via spontaneous categorical responses that the author admits can be unreliable, and reports significance stars in Table 3 without identifying the statistical test. Concurrently, the qualitative strand omits FGD/KII sample sizes, recruitment procedures, data-collection protocols, and any thematic coding or reflexivity account.

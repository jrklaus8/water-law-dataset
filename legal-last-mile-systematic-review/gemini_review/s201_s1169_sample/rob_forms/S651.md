## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Obeng-Odoom (2012) is a single-country political-economy and institutional-historical case study of urban water governance in Ghana (1957-2011) that synthesizes secondary histories, official statistics, scientific water-quality studies, and statutory/regulatory provisions without primary fieldwork, a comparator arm, or a systematic review search protocol. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured non-validated judgment rather than a numeric score), the paper demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity (D2), strong conceptual outcome definition distinguishing MDG coverage ("wide access") from quality, reliability, affordability, and distributional equity ("deep access") (D4), and strong political-economy institutional contextualisation of GWSC/GWCL restructuring, donor conditionality, civil society contestation, CWSA decoupling, and PURC-GWCL-AVRL regulatory asymmetries (D10), alongside adequate PPP exposure classification (D3) and secondary metric reporting (D7). However, methodological rigour for empirical and causal inference is severely constrained by the absence of a documented literature search or source-selection protocol (D5, D6, D11), lack of counterfactual causal identification or confounding adjustment separating the 2005-2011 AVRL management contract from long-standing infrastructure deficits and rapid urban growth (D8, D9), absence of methodological reflexivity (D13), and weak legal/institutional citation precision on p. 1142—where the PURC statute is cited first as Act 538 (1997) and four sentences later as Act 578, and where AVRL's arrangement is repeatedly referred to as affermage despite p. 1139 documenting that affermage was abandoned in favour of a management contract (D1).

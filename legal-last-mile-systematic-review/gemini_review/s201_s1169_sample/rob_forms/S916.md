## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High methodological concern under the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies (1 Unclear, 7 No across Q1-Q8). Toure et al. (2012) conducted a cross-sectional spatial survey of 120 households across a 500 m grid in Mbour, Senegal, mapping SDE piped network connections, well use, income/consumption poverty, water expenditure share, and a custom Water Access Index. While systematic spatial meshing and home-visit surveying achieved high reported participation, major threats to internal and construct validity pervade the study: (1) assigning a single centroid household's responses to an entire 25-hectare urban grid cell without population-density weighting or clear within-cell household selection criteria; (2) conflating household consumption and income while applying an ad hoc 600 CFA franc poverty threshold without reporting survey instrument details; (3) measuring daily water availability differentially (utility bills for paying households vs container/tank capacities for non-paying households) and miscalibrating domestic Water Access Index cutoffs against national total per-capita water withdrawals (0.58 m3/person/day = 580 L/day, classifying up to 250 L/person/day as 'very low access'); (4) erroneously treating 120 households multiplied by a 9.8 national average household size as >1000 independent observations to claim a <=3% margin of error; and (5) inferring water-poverty relationships and 'perverse effects' of the 1995 water sector reform purely from visual map comparisons without statistical tests of association, spatial autocorrelation metrics, or confounding control.

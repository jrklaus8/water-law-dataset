## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Britto, Formiga-Johnsson, and Carneiro (2016) present a multi-source qualitative documentary and interview case study examining water supply inequalities, bulk water availability during the 2014-2015 drought, and hydrosocial scarcity in the Rio de Janeiro Metropolitan Area (RJMA). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits clear research aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative methodology (Q2: Yes) and comparative macro-system case-study design (Q3: Yes), and a coherent, well-structured statement of findings (Q9: Yes) showing that peripheral residential shortages in Baixada Fluminense, São Gonçalo, and Itaboraí stem from incomplete storage/distribution infrastructure, ~40% system losses, and industrial supply prioritization rather than Paraíba do Sul drought alone. However, methodological reporting is weak: participant recruitment (Q4: Can't tell), interview and documentary collection procedures (Q5: Can't tell), and research ethics/consent protocols (Q7: Can't tell) are insufficiently detailed, while researcher reflexivity (Q6: No) and rigorous qualitative data-analysis/coding procedures (Q8: No) are absent beyond juxtaposing prior findings from the three authors. Overall methodological concern is therefore rated Moderate-to-High (3 Can't tell and 2 No items across Q1-Q9).

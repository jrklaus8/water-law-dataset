## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Schwenkel (2015) is a primary qualitative anthropological study—combining nine months of residential participant observation in the East German-built Quang Trung social housing complex in Vinh City across four fieldwork periods (2006, 2009, 2010-11, and 2012) with interviews in Vietnam and Germany, local press analysis, and archival research in Vinh, Hanoi, and Berlin—appraised using the CASP Qualitative Checklist. Methodological strengths include clearly articulated research aims (Q1), an appropriate longitudinal ethnographic and archival design capturing both 1970s-1980s postwar water shortages and contemporary post-privatization overflow/decay (Q2, Q3, Q5), rich researcher reflexivity regarding how the author's own utility consumption and out-of-pocket plumbing repair disrupted collective norms around state liability (Q6), and well-triangulated qualitative findings of high conceptual value for understanding last-mile water/wastewater governance and contested maintenance responsibility (Q9, Q10).

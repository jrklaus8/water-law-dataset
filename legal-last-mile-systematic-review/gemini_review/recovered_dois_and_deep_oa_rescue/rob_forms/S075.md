## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Armstrong, Hope, and Koehler (2022) conduct a comparative longitudinal panel analysis (>3,900 monthly records from 2016–2019 across five operators in Ghana, Rwanda, and Uganda) using service area archetypes and repeated-measures Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) to evaluate how connection type (on-premises vs. off-premises) and payment modality (prepaid credit vs. conventional PAYF and monthly billing) affect volumetric water revenue when controlling for tariff level. Appraised with ROBINS-I, the study has an overall Serious risk of bias. Serious concerns arise in three domains: (D1) Confounding, as GEE regressions adjust only for tariff level, connection type, and payment modality while omitting key socioeconomic, environmental, competing-source, and reliability confounders; (D2) Selection bias, due to convenience sampling of five high-performing, record-keeping operators and post-hoc exclusion of service areas with <12 concurrent months of data or >=2 consecutive zero-revenue months; and (D6) Outcome measurement, because unverified operator logs contain modality-specific measurement distortions—specifically, arrears lumped into on-premises monthly revenue and credit purchase-to-redemption lags in prepaid meter records. Classification of interventions (D3), operational deviations such as informal vendor mark-ups and arrears (D4), and missing data including absent user denominators (D5) pose Moderate risk, while D7 is rated No information in the absence of a pre-registered analysis plan (notwithstanding helpful leave-one-out sensitivity checks).

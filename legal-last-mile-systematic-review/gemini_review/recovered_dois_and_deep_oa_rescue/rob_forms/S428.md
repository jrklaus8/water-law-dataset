## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This analytical cross-sectional survey of 3,947 newer migrant households across 15 notified slums, 15 non-notified slums, and open spaces in Ludhiana, North India (2011–2014) is judged to be of High concern on the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies (Yes: Q1, Q3, Q4; Unclear: Q2, Q7; No: Q5, Q6, Q8). Strengths include clear migration-duration inclusion criteria (30 days to 10 years), random first-stage selection of 15 notified and 15 non-notified slum clusters from Municipal Corporation of Ludhiana lists, and standard Census/NSS definitions for slum and housing categories. However, internal validity and comparative inference between notified and non-notified settlements are substantially weakened by: (1) non-probability purposive and snowball recruitment of households within clusters without reporting non-response rates; (2) absence of demographic/socioeconomic sample descriptors and complete lack of measurement or statistical adjustment for confounders (such as income, duration of stay, and rental vs. owner tenure); (3) unvalidated household-head survey measurement that could not separate formal from unauthorized piped water connections and grouped municipally 'recognized' slums with unrecognized 'identified' slums; and (4) flawed statistical analysis using unadjusted chi-square tests that ignore cluster correlation, exhibit internal degrees-of-freedom contradictions in Tables 3 and 4, and omit the odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals stated in the Methods.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This study reports two sequential field experiments conducted with Nairobi's water and sanitation utility across informal settlements: an individually/compound-randomized tenant engagement outreach experiment (2,584 multi-household compounds) and a two-stage street-cluster-randomized contract enforcement experiment (147 street clusters; 73 treatment clusters and 74 pure control clusters, with compound-level randomization of disconnection notices among 649 eligible compounds inside treatment clusters). Evaluated with the RoB 2 cluster-randomized trials tool, overall risk of bias is rated 'Some concerns', though methodological rigor differs sharply by outcome family. For the primary utility payment and loan-repayment outcomes, risk of bias is Low: pre-intervention identification of compounds and arrears eligibility preceded stratified randomization, baseline balance held across 43 of 45 tests (Appendix Table A3), Intention-to-Treat estimation was used with zero spillover onto untreated compounds (Table 6), and automated electronic billing data (Jisomee Mita) achieved 100% follow-up and objective measurement. 'Some concerns' arise in the secondary 9-month follow-up survey outcomes due to (1) post-intervention recruitment of one tenant per compound when contract enforcement itself increased the share of compounds renting out dwellings by 13.5 percentage points (D1b); (2) non-trivial missingness on meter readings (27%), water expenditure (48.5%), and tenant interviews (~38%) without by-arm attrition tests (D3); (3) unblinded self-reporting of perceptions, water spending/time, mental well-being, and rental income (D4); and (4) omission of collected under-five child health estimates and an unexplained shift from pure-cluster controls (Equation 2) to within-cluster controls in endline survey Tables 7-8 (D5).

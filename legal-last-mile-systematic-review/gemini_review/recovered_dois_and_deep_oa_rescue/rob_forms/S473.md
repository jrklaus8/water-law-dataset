## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Mugisho Nyambwe et al. (2025) conduct a convergent mixed-methods case study of urban sprawl and drinking water services in Bukavu, DR Congo, combining multi-temporal Landsat/Pléiades/SRTM spatial analysis (1980-2024), a cross-sectional survey of 655 households across 67 blocks in 14 districts, archival review of urban planning documents (1927-2021, SDAU, PUR), field observations, and semi-structured interviews using six interview guides with REGIDESO and public officials. Appraised with MMAT (2018) using the quantitative descriptive block (4.1-4.5), overall methodological quality is low (high risk of bias), as MMAT overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component. While the mixed-methods rationale (5.1) and broad descriptive triangulation of spatial expansion, colonial utility capacity deficits, land tenure overlap, and coping strategies (5.3) are informative, both primary empirical strands exhibit major limitations. In the qualitative strand, interviewee counts, selection procedures, interview guide contents, qualitative coding methods, and verbatim supporting quotations are entirely absent (1.2-1.5). In the quantitative strand, blocks were selected purposively with acknowledged exclusion of some peripheral areas (4.2), Formula (1) does not mathematically match n = 655 (4.1), actual non-response is unreported (4.4), key variables ('water stress' and 'Diseases') lack clear operational definitions or household-level linkage to secondary cholera/microbiological data (4.3), statistical reporting relies on unweighted percentages and unadjusted bivariate correlations without confounding control, and the multivariable logistic regression announced in Section 2.3 is completely omitted from Results (4.5).

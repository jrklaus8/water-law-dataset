## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Ribeiro and Galizoni (2003) present a multi-site primary qualitative study (1999-2002) covering 22 rural communities and 188 peasant households across six municipalities in the Upper, Middle, and Lower Jequitinhonha Valley (Minas Gerais, Brazil), contrasting customary rural water norms against formal statutory water governance under Brazil's Federal Water Law (Law No. 9,433/1997). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits clear research aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative ethnographic approach (Q2: Yes), a well-justified multi-subregion field design combining private household interviews with collective community validation debates (Q3: Yes), purposive household variation across water availability and distance to springs (Q4: Yes), and a coherent synthesis of findings on customary non-denial, circulation, spring-water quality hierarchies, gendered labor burdens, and institutional exclusion from basin committees (Q9: Yes). Methodological concerns are moderate overall (3 'Can't tell' items and 1 'No' item): data collection reporting omits recording/transcription procedures, the interview guide text, data saturation, and reconciliation between '3 to 4 families in each community' across 22 communities and the total of 188 interviewed families (Q5: Can't tell); reflexivity regarding power dynamics and potential influence from local NGO, church, and rural union co-interviewers is not critically examined (Q6: Can't tell); formal ethical review, informed consent, and permission to publish participants' real names in Note 1 are unreported (Q7: Can't tell); and analytic rigor is weak because no systematic coding or thematic analysis protocol is described beyond end-of-day oral researcher debriefings, with only three brief unattributed quotes presented across 18 pages and frequent blending of empirical field claims with broader doctrinal-political critique (Q8: No).

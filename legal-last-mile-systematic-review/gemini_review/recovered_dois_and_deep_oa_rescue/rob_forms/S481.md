## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This study combines a non-systematic legal/institutional synthesis of rural water supply governance in Brazil (highlighting Law No. 11.445/2007, Law No. 14.026/2020, PNSR, PLANSAB, and Ceará's SISAR co-management model under Complementary Law No. 162/2016) with a small descriptive expert-elicitation survey (N=11 specialists interviewed face-to-face at a May 2016 conference in Fortaleza, Ceará, voting binary yes/no on 14 multiscale governance strategies with an >80% retention threshold). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment), the study offers an adequate descriptive mapping of multiscale institutional competencies across national, regional/municipal, and community association tiers (D2, D3, D7, D10). However, its empirical evidence base is methodologically weak: specialists were convenience-sampled by self-interest at a Ceará seminar to evaluate Ceará/SISAR-inspired proposals (high selection/allegiance bias, D6); demographic reporting in Section 2.3 contains arithmetic inconsistencies (D5); legal/background citations contain errors such as misattributing Law No. 11.445 to a Law No. 14.026 citation and stating '1 billion Brazilians' lack sanitation (D1); literature synthesis and post-evaluation item revision procedures are undocumented (D11, D12, D13); and unblinded expert opinion is conflated with strategy effectiveness without any empirical measurement of water supply sustainability outcomes or counterfactual comparison (D4, D8, D9).

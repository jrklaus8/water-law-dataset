## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Feler and Henderson (2008) present an ambitious comparative panel and instrumental-variables evaluation of strategic municipal under-servicing of informal/low-income housing across Brazilian urban localities (Minimum Comparable Areas, primarily 351 MCAs in 55 multi-locality urban areas) using 1970, 1980, 1991, and 2000 IBGE Population Census data. Appraised with ROBINS-I, the study is judged at Serious overall risk of bias, driven primarily by confounding and exclusion-restriction vulnerabilities (D1: Serious). Although Section 5 addresses reverse causality and growth-backlog bias in OLS via urban-area fixed effects and 2SLS/LIML instruments (porous geology interacted with mean and SD of insolation, plus 1970 historical locality/rest-of-metro covariates), the authors themselves note that geological and insolation instruments may correlate with contemporary private water-supply costs (as well as construction/amenity conditions), multi-endogenous-variable first-stage strength is modest (partial F ~ 13, and 6.2 when using untitled homes), and Section 4's spatial-reaction estimates rely on uninstrumented lagged covariates after external instruments proved weak. Moderate concerns also arise in sample selection (D2: excluding <50% urbanized peripheral MCAs where deflected informal settlements may locate), intervention classification (D3: proxying intentional municipal exclusionary policy via census central-water connection rates of 1-2 or 1-3 room houses across municipal/regional utilities), and post-1991 policy deviations/co-interventions (D4: widespread post-1988 national slum-upgrading, regularization, and local minimum lot-size laws during 1991-2000). Missing data (D5) and census outcome measurement (D6) pose low risk, while selection of the reported result (D7) is rated No information in the absence of a pre-analysis plan (noting also that tables/figures on PDF pp. 39-46 and 48-50 were unextracted image objects in the cached PDF, though all narrative/econometric prose on pp. 1-38 and 47 was readable).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This primary qualitative case study investigates domestic water access, household coping strategies, and customary environmental/water bylaws in Nanka, an erosion- and landslide-prone rural community in Anambra State, Nigeria. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits moderate methodological concern (2 'No' and 2 'Can't tell' items across Q1-Q9). Strengths include clearly articulated aims, appropriate multi-source qualitative fieldwork (140 semi-structured interviews across households, traditional/community leaders, leaders of thought, and government officials, triangulated with field observations and documentary/bylaw evidence), and coherent findings illuminating how communal erosion-control rules (mandatory stormwater ditches 'mgbacha ogwugwu', bans on boreholes/wells, and sacred-stream taboos) constrain groundwater use and channel households toward rainwater storage, water sharing, stream fetching, and sachet/tanker vendors. Key limitations are the absence of researcher reflexivity despite gatekeeper-mediated access via sanction-enforcing village/clan heads (Q6), declaring IRB approval and informed consent 'Not applicable' for 140 human-subjects interviews (Q7), incomplete reporting of within-village household selection mechanics, refusals, and exclusion of households without dependents (Q4), and thin analytical transparency with only four verbatim quotes and occasional denominator ambiguities in descriptive interview percentages (Q8).

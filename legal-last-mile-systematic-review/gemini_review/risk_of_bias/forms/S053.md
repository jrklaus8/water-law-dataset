## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Loewenson et al. (2023) is a multi-method qualitative study combining a focused (explicitly non-systematic) narrative desk review of 52 post-2010 English-language documents across 16 East and Southern African countries with 10 purposively selected city case studies (Harare, Kampala, Lusaka, and Nairobi) informed by 122 published/grey documents and 34 IRB-cleared key informant interviews, synthesized via manual thematic content analysis across five equity dimensions. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study demonstrates clear research aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative multi-case design (Q2: Yes; Q3: Yes), documented NYU IRB clearance, informed consent, and informant anonymisation (Q7: Yes), and well-structured cross-case findings (Q9: Yes) with substantial regional policy relevance (Q10). However, there is moderate methodological concern overall (3 'Can't tell' items and 1 'No' item): recruitment details and stakeholder composition/refusals for the 34 key informants are unreported in this paper (Q4: Can't tell); primary interview procedures (interview guide, recording/transcription, saturation) are omitted and field focus groups/observations were precluded by resource constraints (Q5: Can't tell); researcher reflexivity is absent despite city focal persons and the sponsoring EQUINET network having direct involvement in selecting, implementing, or supporting studied initiatives (Q6: No); and manual coding was conducted by one lead author without a reported codebook or primary verbatim participant quotes in the text (Q8: Can't tell). Legal/institutional features and WASH/health outcomes are summarized descriptively from secondary reports without primary doctrinal or quantitative verification.

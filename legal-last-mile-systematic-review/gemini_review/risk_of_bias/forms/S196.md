## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This legal-institutional multiple-case docket study tracks the procedural history and post-judgment enforcement (cumprimento de sentença) of three Brazilian Public Civil Actions (ações civis públicas) demanding municipal sewerage infrastructure in Aquidauana (MS), Santa Maria (RS), and Tubarão (SC), drawn from the 258 lawsuits previously mapped in Barcellos (2014). Under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment), the study is strong in legal source accuracy, jurisdictional specificity, procedural exposure definition, institutional context, and docket-level replicability: it documents exact docket numbers, judicial timelines, appellate delays, daily fine orders, and prosecutorial enforcement failures, notably exposing how the Aquidauana proceeding was formally extinguished as "fulfilled" in 2011 upon submission of a 30-year concession bill while actual municipal sewage coverage stood at 15%, and how Tubarão lacked sewerage implementation 12 years after filing. However, substantial methodological limitations restrict generalizability and causal inference: the selection of 3 cases from 258 is asserted to be "random" without any documented sampling method; docket synthesis relies on unpublished 2016 undergraduate chronicles without a standardized coding rubric; restricted online access to full court files in TJMS and TJRS limited documentary verification (and Santa Maria's 2012 compliance was inferred solely from judicial archiving without external physical audit); and the descriptive design lacks comparator municipalities or controls for concurrent concession renewals, licensing bottlenecks, and municipal politics.

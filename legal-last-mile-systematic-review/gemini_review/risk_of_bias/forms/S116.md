## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Castleden et al. (2017) report a combined systematic and realist review (SRR) of 63 peer-reviewed and grey-literature documents (out of 279 retrieved records) examining integrative Indigenous and Western knowledge systems in Canadian water research and management. Appraised with AMSTAR 2, overall methodological confidence is Critically Low due to four critical flaws (Q2, Q7, Q9, Q13) alongside four non-critical weaknesses (Q6, Q8, Q10, Q16). Methodological strengths include co-design of eligibility criteria and a detailed 33-item extraction framework with a National Advisory Committee and national Water Gatherings, multi-database academic and grey-literature searches with published search strings, two-stage independent screening for Scopus and Web of Science records, and thoughtful realist discussion of contextual, disciplinary, and treaty/governance variation. However, the review lacked a registered or published a priori protocol (Q2), excluded French-language Canadian scholarship (Q4 Partial Yes), divided data extraction across reviewers rather than extracting in duplicate (Q6), provided neither a list of excluded full-text studies (Q7) nor a study-by-study table or bibliography clearly identifying all 63 included records and their funding sources (Q8, Q10), omitted any risk-of-bias or methodological quality assessment of primary empirical studies and thus could not incorporate study quality into conclusions (Q9, Q13), left database screening counts partially unreconciled in text (226 retrieved vs. 40 included and 64 excluded), and omitted a conflict-of-interest disclosure despite author overlap with cited primary studies (Q16). While AMSTAR 2 is calibrated strictly for interventional reviews, the absence of study-level reporting tables, excluded-study lists, duplicate extraction, and primary-study quality appraisal also represents substantial transparency and bias limitations under realist/qualitative synthesis standards.

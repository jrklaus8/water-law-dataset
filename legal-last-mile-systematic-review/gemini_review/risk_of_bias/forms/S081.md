## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

S081 (Dasgupta & Agarwal, 2022) is a primary qualitative participatory practice-research (action-research) case study examining an experimental urban-rural local government partnership for faecal sludge management (FSM) in Dhenkanal district, Odisha, India (2019-2021), where Dhenkanal Municipality and 17 neighbouring gram panchayats signed a Memorandum of Agreement (MoA) on 28 December 2020. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits moderate to high methodological concern (4 Yes, 3 Can't tell, and 2 No items across Q1-Q9). Its strengths lie in its clear research aims (Q1), appropriate qualitative practice-research framing (Q2, Q3), and lucid institutional analysis of India's constitutional decentralization architecture, spatial boundary mismatches across non-contiguous gram panchayats, accountability deficits in non-binding intergovernmental MoAs, and tariff surcharge equity (Q9, Q10). However, methodological rigor is substantially weakened by unreported recruitment/selection procedures for the 8 interviewed sarpanches and consultation participants (Q4), undocumented data collection protocols (Q5), absence of IRB review justification (Q7), lack of a transparent qualitative analytic/coding procedure (Q8), absence of researcher reflexivity despite the authors' embedded role as BMGF-funded technical advisors co-designing the pilot (Q6), and unsubstantiated Abstract claims of increased rural service access without post-MoA empirical uptake data.

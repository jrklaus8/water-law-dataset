## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This primary qualitative study (focused ethnography between 2021 and 2023 in downtown Juiz de Fora, Brazil, combining field observations, statutory/policy document analysis, and semi-structured interviews with 24 informal street vendors and 6 municipal managers) was appraised using the CASP Qualitative Checklist. Methodological quality raises moderate concern overall (8 'Yes', 2 'Can't tell', 0 'No' across Q1-Q9, plus strong substantive value in Q10). Strengths include clearly articulated human-rights accountability aims, effective triangulation of manager and vendor testimonies with primary municipal statutes/bylaws (Law No. 14.290/2021, 2022 Municipal Sanitation Plan, CESAMA Bylaws) and direct facility observations, formal ethics approval, and structured qualitative content analysis (Schreier, 2012) with documented two-wave intra-coder reliability (>80% agreement; Krippendorff's alpha >0.8). Main limitations are incomplete reporting of street-vendor recruitment details (seed selection, demographic/occupational composition, refusal rates, and an unexplained 'CH_3' participant code on PDF p. 7), reliance on social-network snowballing in a single convenience-selected city, single-coder analysis without independent inter-coder verification, and minimal critical reflexivity beyond noting the first author's residential familiarity and lack of prior contact with participants.

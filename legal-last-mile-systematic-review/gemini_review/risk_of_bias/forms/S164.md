## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

García Salazar and Lara Figueroa (2024) conduct a doctrinal-empirical compliance study evaluating the Human Right to Water (DHA) across all 84 municipalities of Hidalgo, Mexico. Doctrinally and contextually, the paper is strong: it accurately grounds its evaluation in international norms (UN General Comment No. 15, UNGA Res. 64/292), Mexican constitutional provisions (Arts. 4 and 115), federal water law (Ley de Aguas Nacionales, REPDA), and 2022 state-enacted municipal tariff decrees (decrees 55-170), while situating findings within Hidalgo's complex institutional reality (intermittent tandeo delivery, CAASIM intermunicipal management, and over a century of untreated ZMVM wastewater irrigation in the Mezquital Valley). Methodologically, however, its empirical operationalization exhibits major measurement, coding, and inferential limitations: per-capita water availability is proxied by legally concessioned REPDA volumes rather than actual water delivered to homes (ignoring leakage and tandeo rationing, producing paradoxically high availability in rural municipalities with high shares of unpiped dwellings); household affordability combines undocumented single-tariff extractions from municipal decrees with a uniform 2017 national bottled-water expenditure mean and hypothetical 1- or 2-minimum-wage incomes rather than observed household budgets; internal unit/denominator inconsistencies appear on p. 178 and in Table 4; and purely descriptive accounting cannot support causal claims or control for municipal socioeconomic and geographic confounding.

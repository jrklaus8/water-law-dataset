## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Jackson and Palmer (2012) present a comparative legal-institutional and policy case study examining the interface between statutory water governance reforms ('modernisation') and Indigenous/customary water governance in Australia and East Timor. Because the article has no primary participant recruitment, interview protocol, or systematic literature search—instead combining doctrinal/policy analysis of statutes, intergovernmental agreements, and draft water legislation with published ethnographic and donor-evaluation literature—it is appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework as a structured non-validated judgment (with no numeric score). Methodological strengths lie in its strong jurisdictional specificity (D2) and rich institutional, historical, and cosmological contextualization (D10), supported by adequate legal source accuracy (D1) and exposure characterization (D3), including direct analysis of Australia's Native Title Act 1993, 2004 National Water Initiative, and East Timor's unpublished 2012 Draft Water Resources Law. Conversely, empirical rigour is weak across sampling (D5), case/source selection (D6), outcome definition (D4), measurement transparency (D7), causal identification (D8), treatment of confounding (D9), coding transparency (D12), replication potential (D11), and researcher reflexivity (D13), due to the absence of a documented methodology, reliance on secondary estimates and personal communication, and lack of a counterfactual design.

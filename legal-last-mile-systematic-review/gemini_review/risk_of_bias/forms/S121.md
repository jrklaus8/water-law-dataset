## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Feler and Henderson (2008) conduct a non-randomized comparative longitudinal/panel econometric evaluation across 351 urban Minimum Comparable Areas (MCAs) in 55 multi-locality Brazilian urban areas (1970–2000 IBGE Censuses), examining (1) strategic municipal under-provision of central piped water to small (1–3 room) houses in 1991 using urban-area fixed effects, lagged OLS, and spatial lag/error MLE, and (2) the causal effect of 1991 small-house water servicing on 1991–2000 urban household growth and educational composition using 2SLS and LIML instrumental variables (porous geology, insolation interactions, and 1970 historical census/market-access variables). Appraised with ROBINS-I, overall risk of bias is Serious, driven by two Serious-risk domains: D1 (Confounding) and D3 (Classification of interventions). Specifically, deliberate exclusionary policy is not directly measured from municipal ordinances or utility refusal records but is proxied ecologically by census piped-water coverage among all 1–3 room dwellings (which conflates intentional exclusion with rapid-growth infrastructure backlogs); furthermore, in the IV growth regressions, geological and historical 1970 instruments face plausible exclusion-restriction violations and only modest multi-endogenous first-stage strength (partial F ~ 13, dropping to 6.2 when water coverage of untitled homes is used), while spatial reaction-function estimates are sensitive to weight-matrix specification. Strengths include harmonized MCA boundaries preventing municipal-split attrition (Low risk in D5), standardized decennial census outcome measurement (Low risk in D6), and informative falsification checks (1991 large houses and post-democratization 2000 small houses).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Paludo and Borba (2013) present a comparative institutional-historical case study of two post-CASAN municipal water supply and sanitation governance models implemented between 2003 and 2009 in Santa Catarina, Brazil: shared public-participatory management ('Gestão Compartilhada') in Indaial versus a 25-year private concession ('Gestão Privatizada') in Itapema. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured non-validated judgment), the study demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity, concrete operationalization of municipal legal-institutional regimes (statutes, council/fund structures, social tariffs, tender notices, and litigation), and rich political-institutional contextualization—including documenting institutional persistence after partisan alternation in both municipalities. However, methodological concerns are substantial regarding causal identification, confounding, and outcome measurement: with N=2 purposively selected municipalities, endogenous socio-economic and partisan selection into governance models (industrial economy and strong civil society in Indaial vs. seasonal coastal tourism and political turmoil in Itapema), unstandardized documentary/newspaper coding, and asymmetric secondary data bases ('os dados partem de bases diferentes') lacking baseline-to-endline parity across cases, descriptive differences in unit water cost (R$2.1 vs. R$2.7/m³) and coverage cannot be causally attributed to the legal management model alone.

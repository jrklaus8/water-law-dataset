## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Behl et al. (2022) conducted a primary mixed-methods pre-post evaluation of the Asili social enterprise program (small-format primary care clinics and clean water standpipes) in two conflict-affected communities (Mudaka and Panzi) in South Kivu, Democratic Republic of Congo. Appraised using the MMAT (2018), overall methodological quality is low (high methodological concern), as MMAT guidance stipulates that overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component. While the study productively integrates exploratory quantitative k-means market segmentation of household wealth proxies with qualitative focus group insights to show that user fees limited Asili uptake among poorer households, both empirical strands exhibit major limitations. Quantitatively, the study was retrospectively registered (NCT03536286) near follow-up, suffered severe longitudinal attrition (66% loss in Mudaka; 42% loss in Panzi), dropped its intended primary child morbidity outcomes entirely due to poor maternal recall and unavailable clinic charts, relied on self-reported Asili membership/usage affected by respondent confusion between 'member' and 'user', selectively omitted baseline water/sanitation cluster variables lacking 'notable trends', and performed no control-group or multivariable confounder adjustment. Qualitatively, the Methods section omits the number of focus groups, participant selection criteria, interview guides, recording/transcription procedures, and formal coding methodology.

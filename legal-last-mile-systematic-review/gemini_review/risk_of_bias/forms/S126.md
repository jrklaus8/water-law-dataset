## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Murphy et al. (2015) report an exploratory sequential mixed-methods study of First Nations drinking water operators in Canada, combining semi-structured qualitative interviews in 8 southern Ontario First Nations communities (March–August 2014) with a subsequent descriptive cross-sectional questionnaire completed by 79 First Nations water treatment operators at a provincial conference in British Columbia (October 2014). Appraised using the MMAT (2018) with the Quantitative Descriptive block (items 4.1–4.5), the study has a clear objective and coherent sequential design in which Ontario qualitative interviews directly informed the BC questionnaire and joint policy synthesis. However, per MMAT guidance that overall methodological quality cannot exceed the weakest component, the study carries high methodological concern. In the qualitative component, only 8 of 45 listed (28 confirmed) southern Ontario 'best case' communities participated, interviews were hand-recorded on paper rather than audio-recorded due to fear of repercussions, coding methods are described in a single generic sentence, and almost no verbatim quotes from Ontario interviews are presented. In the quantitative component, BC respondents were a convenience sample of attendees at an 'Operational Excellence' conference with no reported total attendance denominator (unknown response rate) and an unverified assumption that each operator represented a distinct community; measurements relied on an unvalidated self-report survey that allowed respondents to check both 'satisfied' and 'unsatisfied', exhibited an internal arithmetic error in salary frequencies (50 + 16 + 33 = 99 reported responses out of N = 79), and omitted x-axis category labels in published Figures 3 and 5. No objective water quality or administrative records were collected, and confounding by community remoteness, plant complexity, or revenue capacity was not controlled.

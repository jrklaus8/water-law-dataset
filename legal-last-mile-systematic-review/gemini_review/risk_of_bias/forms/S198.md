## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Greene Dobbins (2019) combines a philosophical and historical essay on the human right to water and sanitation (Sections 1-5) with an empirical regulatory and institutional case study of wastewater governance failure in Alabama's Black Belt, focusing in depth on the municipal sewer lagoon and spray-field system in Uniontown, Perry County (Section 6). Because the paper evaluates primary legal-administrative enforcement records (ADEM Notices of Violation, a 2005 civil complaint, a 2008 judicial/administrative Consent Decree, NPDES permits, a 2014 Stop Work Order, 76 non-compliance citations, USEPA's 2013 Diagnostic Evaluation Report, and municipal engineering filings) alongside collated sanitary sewer overflow (SSO) data and author field observations—without a comparator arm, formal qualitative interview/ethnographic methodology, quantitative survey/cohort design, or systematic literature search—it is appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework. As a structured non-validated judgment, institutional contextualization (D10) and jurisdictional specificity (D2) are strong, and legal-administrative source documentation and exposure/outcome definitions within the Uniontown case study are adequate (D1, D3, D4). However, overall empirical rigor is weak due to the absence of a Methods section or coding/sampling protocol (D5, D12), purposive selection of an extreme failure case without a counterfactual comparator (D6, D8), inability to disentangle regulatory forbearance from severe fiscal, industrial-loading, and clay-soil constraints (D9), reliance on unmetered municipal SSO self-reports and undocumented 'unpublished data' / 'personal observations' by the author for microbiological and field claims (D7, D11), and lack of researcher reflexivity (D13).

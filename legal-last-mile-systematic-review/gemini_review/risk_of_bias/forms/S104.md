## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This analytical cross-sectional study (n = 3,047 urban and peri-urban piped-water households across 177 clusters in the 2018 Zambia Demographic and Health Survey, spatially linked to 2017 IBNET utility data and 2018 NWASCO statutory regulator data across 11 commercial Water and Sewerage Companies) is methodologically transparent and appropriately uses two-level multilevel logistic regression to separate household-, neighbourhood-, and provider-level variation in self-reported drinking-water interruptions. Using the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies, overall methodological concern is rated as 'Some concern' (6 'Yes' items: Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8; 2 'Unclear' items: Q3, Q7). Key strengths include a systematic 54-country data audit justifying Zambia's selection, explicit sample exclusion accounting, checks for multicollinearity and cluster-level variance (ICC), adjustment for provider fixed effects (Model 1) and contextual covariates (Model 2), and open data/code availability. Primary sources of risk of bias are measurement limitations in both the outcome and contextual exposures—specifically, reliance on retrospective 2-week self-reports of >=24-hour outages (omitting shorter daily rationing and vulnerable to recall bias), unverified utility self-reporting underlying IBNET and NWASCO indicators with unclear service-hour definitions, temporal and definitional mismatches across linked secondary datasets (2010 DHS urban/rural frame, 2015 provincial GDP and GHSL grids, 2017 IBNET, and 2018 NWASCO/DHS), omission of rural and informal/small-scale piped supplies, unmeasured confounders (such as household water storage tanks), and reliance on backward stepwise variable selection without a pre-registered analysis plan.

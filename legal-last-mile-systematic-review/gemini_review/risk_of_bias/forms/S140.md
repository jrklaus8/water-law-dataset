## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Jacob and Kotzebue (2026) present a single-site convergent mixed-methods community case study evaluating post-upgrading living conditions in Bamboo Settlement #3 (379 lots), Trinidad and Tobago, against the five UN-Habitat SDG 11.1 dimensions (improved water, improved sanitation, sufficient living space, structural quality/durability, and secure tenure). Appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018, using Quantitative Descriptive items 4.1-4.5 alongside Qualitative 1.1-1.5 and Mixed-Methods 5.1-5.5), the study exhibits low methodological quality / high risk of bias. While the research questions, UN-Habitat conceptual framework, lot-based simple random sampling frame, and pragmatic rationale for mixing methods are clearly stated (S1, S2, 1.1, 4.1, 5.1 = Yes), both empirical strands have substantial flaws. Quantitatively, only 31 of 66 sampled lots participated (47% response rate; 4.2, 4.4 = No), creating severe non-response bias in a setting where tenure-insecure residents may avoid surveyors; household water affordability (<10% of income) was inferred from county-level census income rather than surveyed household income (4.3 = Can't tell); planned one-sample t-tests were never reported; and basic arithmetic contradictions pervade the results (e.g., 14 formal titles + 2 Certificates of Comfort + 10 HDC/landowner agreements = 26, reported as 25 secure and 5 insecure on p. 7, then as 26 secure and 25 insecure on p. 8, alongside a contradictory claim on p. 7 that most households met all five dimensions despite zero households meeting the floodplain/location requirement; 4.5 = No). Qualitatively, relying solely on open-ended items in a 35-question digital survey without verbatim quotes or reflexivity contradicts the claimed Braun and Clarke reflexive thematic analysis (1.2, 1.4, 1.5 = No; 1.3 = Can't tell), and integration was restricted to quantitizing preliminary 'HDC engagement' into binary tenure counts (5.2-5.5 = No). Furthermore, without baseline or comparator data, the study cannot causally isolate state upgrading/regularization effects from resident self-help or locational advantages.

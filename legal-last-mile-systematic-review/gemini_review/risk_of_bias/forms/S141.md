## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Chumo et al. (2023) report a primary qualitative participatory study (FGDs and multi-stakeholder workshops with 200 childcare managers and parents across Korogocho and Viwandani informal settlements in Nairobi) documenting the co-creation and stakeholder self-evaluation of informal social accountability mechanisms (iSAMs) for WASH delivery in childcare centers. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, methodological reporting is strong across 8 of the 9 binary/ternary items (Q1-Q5, Q7-Q9 rated Yes): aims, participatory design, purposive sampling across village units, multi-stage data collection, multi-board ethical approvals, third-party transcript verification, NVivo framework analysis with inter-coder agreement, and balanced reporting of positive and negative appraisals are all clearly documented. The primary methodological weakness is Q6 (Can't tell): the authors do not critically examine researcher reflexivity, long-standing institutional presence (APHRC/NUHDSS), or facilitator-induced social desirability bias when participants evaluated workshops led by the research team. In addition, purposive selection of longest-tenured, dedicated parents limits representativeness, longitudinal workshop retention is not quantified, and WASH service improvements were captured solely as participant perceptions rather than verified facility or health outcomes.

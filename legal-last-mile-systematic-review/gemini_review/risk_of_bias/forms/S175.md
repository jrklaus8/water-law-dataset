## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Bellaubi and Pahl-Wostl (2017) present a deterministic, game-theoretic agent-based model (ABM) implemented in NetLogo v4.1.3 to explore how corruption risks (operationalized via transparency, accountability, participation [TAP], and social cost/gain scores) and management practices (opportunistic vs. pragmatic) shape urban water service delivery (WSD) performance in Kenya and Ghana. Because the paper conducts a computational social simulation parameterized and calibrated with pre-existing summary scores from five previously published urban case studies (Bellaubi and Visscher 2014, 2016; GII 2011; TIK 2011) rather than primary empirical data collection, doctrinal/jurimetric legal analysis, or a systematic review, no listed risk-of-bias checklist applies (chosen_tool = NONE). Methodologically, depositing the model's ODD documentation in the CoMSES library (Bellaubi et al. 2014) supports computational transparency. However, substantial evidentiary limitations constrain empirical and causal inference: (1) all TAP, social-cost, and WSDA performance inputs are imported from secondary 'participatory scoring' and 'personal observation' without primary sampling, coding, or reliability details in this text; (2) Table 3 swaps the definitions and measurement units of Continuity and Coverage, and Table 5 cites a maximum indicator score of 3 (total 15) that contradicts Table 3's 0-2 scoring scale; (3) payoff functional forms (e.g., T = 1.5 - accountability; S = (participation / C) - social cost) and sequential move order (agent always moving first, preventing shifting Cd/Dc dynamics at the consumption level; PDF p. 12) are imposed by assumption without structural sensitivity testing; and (4) calibration tunes one free parameter C per country (1.6 in Kenya; 1.8 in Ghana) to match a single unweighted country-mean WSDA score (23.3% across 3 Kenyan sites; 16.6% across 2 Ghanaian sites), providing no independent out-of-sample validation and leaving physical, financial, and hydrological confounders unmodeled.

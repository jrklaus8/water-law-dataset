## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Enqvist and van Oyen (2023) employ Q-methodology—an inherently mixed-methods ('qualiquantological') design combining qualitative concourse generation and crib-sheet discourse interpretation with quantitative forced-grid ranking and principal component analysis (PCA)—to explore subjective perceptions of water tariff fairness across three socioeconomically and historically distinct Cape Town neighbourhoods (City Bowl, Mitchells Plain and Dunoon) 1 year after the 2015–2018 drought. Appraised using the MMAT (2018), the study demonstrates strong conceptual coherence, careful piloting of a 25-statement Q-set, transparent PCA/varimax factor extraction (p < 0.01 loading threshold of ±0.516; 5 multi-respondent factors explaining 61% of variance), effective integration of factor arrays with household socioeconomic profiles and measured interpretation that explicitly avoids causal or population-representative claims. Methodological concerns stem from convenience and snowball sampling (30 interviewed, 27 usable Q-sorts) with pronounced workplace clustering (all four 'Individualist' Factor 2 respondents worked at a single school in Mitchells Plain), unreported initial refusal rates and reasons for excluding 3 unusable Q-sorts, absence of verbatim post-sort qualitative interview quotes to substantiate why respondents ranked statements as they did (leaving historical/interactional explanations reliant on secondary literature) and lack of reported ethical approval/consent or researcher reflexivity across multilingual, highly unequal study sites. Per MMAT 2018 guidance, no numeric composite score is computed; overall quality reflects moderate methodological concern bounded by these sampling and qualitative reporting limitations.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High concern under the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies (3 items rated No [Q4, Q6, Q8] and 5 items rated Unclear [Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7]). Lima et al. (2022) conducted a quantitative cross-sectional perception survey of 393 households across 7 census-sector groups defined by a 2010 Census Social Development Index (SDI) in the Santa Rosa neighborhood of Guarujá (São Paulo, Brazil), spatializing descriptive findings in GIS and contextualizing them against federal and municipal basic sanitation laws and plans. Key methodological limitations include: (1) an unweighted disproportionate stratified block-sampling design (sampling fractions ranging from 2.3% in Group 4 to 75.0% in Group 7) paired with a simple random sample formula assuming a 12% margin of error, undocumented within-block/household respondent selection rules, unreported non-response rates, and omission of fieldwork dates and respondent demographics; (2) reliance on an unvalidated 9-item closed-ended perception instrument (Appendix I) without objective service verification or operational definitions of 'Adequate' vs. 'Precarious', alongside an incomplete 2010 SDI index missing two education indicators and borrowing weights from Rio de Janeiro; and (3) purely descriptive unweighted percentage comparisons without confounder adjustment, inferential statistics, or reconciliation of numerical inconsistencies between the narrative text/Figure 3 (pp. 13-14) and Table 4 (p. 18) for sanitation coverage and adequacy.

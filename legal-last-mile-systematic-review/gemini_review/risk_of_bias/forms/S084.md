## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This analytical cross-sectional survey of 593 randomly sampled households (out of 600 approached; 98.8% completeness) across two neighbourhoods of the Mandala slum in Mumbai exhibits strong sampling execution within the target neighbourhoods, previously validated two-week physical container enumeration of household water consumption (LPCD), direct GIS mapping of community water tap functionality, and multivariable adjustment for household size, income, and religion. Overall methodological concern is rated as 'Some concern' rather than 'Low concern' for three reasons: (1) legal status ('notified' vs 'non-notified') was operationalized ecologically by neighbourhood boundary using resident researchers' ground knowledge rather than verifying household-level pre-2000 eligibility documents under the Maharashtra Slum Areas Act; (2) comparing a single notified neighbourhood (Matangrushi Nagar) against a single adjacent non-notified neighbourhood (Indira Nagar) leaves neighbourhood legal status spatially collinear with settlement age and proximity to the main road vs river/landfill, without cluster-adjusted inference; and (3) cross-sectional measurement precludes causal identification and omits microbiological water quality testing as well as survey differentiation between piped municipal taps and brackish borewell taps.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Structured non-validated judgment (no numeric composite score computed): Weak overall methodological rigor. Pastrana-Miranda and González-Caamal (2022) present a neoinstitutional historical-documentary analysis and single-municipality case study (Ecatepec) examining how centre-periphery territorial marginalization, intergovernmental centralization, irregular land tenure, and partisan clientelism shape inequitable water distribution in the Metropolitan Zone of the Valley of Mexico (ZMVM). Strengths include informative political-historical contextualization of hydraulic transfers (Lerma and Cutzamala), intergovernmental asymmetries between CDMX, Estado de México (CAEM), and municipal utilities (SAPASE), and descriptive spatial mapping of municipal piped-water unavailability from the 2015 INEGI Encuesta Intercensal (Figures 1-2). However, the article lacks a dedicated Methods section, relies on secondary literature rather than primary statutes or administrative records (despite claiming primary documentary sources in the abstract, and misdating LGEEPA to 2014 while omitting federal CONAGUA statutory rules), purposively selects Ecatepec partly for media visibility, and reports 7 water-protest episodes from 2018-2020 (Table 1) without disclosing newspaper sources, search strategies, inclusion criteria, or coding rules. Causal claims attributing peripheral water scarcity to territorial marginalization and electoral clientelism are not isolated from hydrological, demographic, and unplanned-settlement confounders.

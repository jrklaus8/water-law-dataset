## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Structured non-validated appraisal under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework indicates high methodological concern / weak empirical rigor alongside strong institutional contextualization. Xerente and Naval (2026) combine a PRISMA-framed Scopus literature search (44 included articles, not individually listed), a doctrinal review of 10 Brazilian federal constitutional, statutory, and regulatory norms (Table 2), and descriptive secondary administrative data from SESAI/SIASI (2019) covering Indigenous villages in DSEI-Tocantins (Tables 3-4). While the study offers strong institutional and jurisdictional contextualization (D2, D10) and generally adequate identification of Brazilian sanitation and Indigenous health legislation (D1, though Table 2 mislabels Ministry of Health Portaria No. 755/2012 as Law No. 12,651), its empirical component exhibits substantial weaknesses across exposure and outcome operationalization, sampling, measurement, causal inference, confounding, replication, and reporting (D3-D9, D11-D12). Despite stating in Methods that primary quantitative and qualitative village data were collected and analyzed with chi-square and t-tests (p<0.05), the Results present zero primary qualitative data and zero statistical test results, omit 4 of the 9 DSEI-Tocantins ethnic groups from Tables 3-4 without explanation, contain text-table transcription errors (e.g., reporting 51 ADD cases on p. 6 vs 131 in Table 4, and glossing SAA as 'Alternative Health Actions'), and rely on ADD surveillance data subject to acknowledged under-notification in unserved villages.

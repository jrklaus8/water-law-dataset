## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Pulgarín-Franco (2024) conducts a non-randomized longitudinal pre-post (uncontrolled before-after) administrative evaluation across the 53 municipalities of Caldas, Quindío, and Risaralda (Colombia), testing whether municipal cadastral updating changes property tax collection, national water/sanitation intergovernmental transfers (SGP), aqueduct and sewerage subscribers and coverage, and drinking-water quality risk (IRCA) across 5-year pre-update versus 5-year post-update windows. Appraised with ROBINS-I, the study is at Critical overall risk of bias. First, confounding bias (D1) is Critical: each municipality is analyzed separately with a univariate robust paired-samples t-test (df = 2 or 3) over a 10-year span without an unexposed comparator group, detrending, inflation adjustment, or control for urbanization, SGP statutory allocation formulas, municipal revenue code reforms, or tax-arrears recovery campaigns. Second, bias due to missing data (D5) and outcome measurement (D6) are both Serious: missing SUI and IRCA records exclude 37.7% of municipalities (20/53) from aqueduct/sewerage analyses and 20.8% (11/53) from water quality analyses; SUI subscriber series exhibit acknowledged outliers in 20 of 33 analyzed municipalities (yielding t = -Inf and NaN effect sizes in Table 6); utility coverage relies on a custom proxy combining SUI subscribers with cadastral parcel counts (whose Formula 1 is missing from the published page on PDF p. 7); urban and rural subscribers cannot be separated even where cadastral updates were urban-only or rural-only; and IRCA scores reflect changing health-authority water-sampling intensity. Selection of municipalities into sub-analyses (D2), intervention classification (D3), co-interventions (D4), and incomplete/truncated results tables without multiplicity adjustment (D7) pose Moderate concerns, though the author candidly reports predominantly null findings on aqueduct coverage and water quality.

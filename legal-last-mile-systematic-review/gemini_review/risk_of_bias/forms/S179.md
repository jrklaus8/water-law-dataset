## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Cord et al. (2022) is a primary qualitative study combining qualitative documentary analysis of 9 Ugandan water and decentralization laws/policies (1997-2019) with semi-structured interviews of 93 district and sub-county technical (46) and political (47) local government actors across 22 sub-counties in 3 Ugandan districts. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study demonstrates low-to-moderate methodological concern (7 'Yes', 2 'Can't tell', 0 'No' on Q1-Q9, and high value on Q10). Strengths include clearly articulated research questions grounded in Organizational Institutional Theory and the Service Delivery Cycle, comprehensive coding of Uganda's statutory and policy framework, large and balanced interview coverage of both elected and appointed local officials, independent Ugandan interviewers, rigorous double-coding in NVivo 12 with documented inter-coder agreement (Cohen's Kappa = 0.78), thorough IRB and confidentiality safeguards, and nuanced reporting of divergent perspectives. The two 'Can't tell' ratings reflect (1) incomplete reporting of sampling/recruitment mechanics and non-response rates across the 3 districts and 22 sub-counties (Q4), and (2) partial reflexivity (Q6): although independent local research assistants conducted the interviews and co-authors' employment with Whave Solutions (an Area Service Provider active in all three study districts) was disclosed, the authors did not critically examine how Whave Solutions co-authors and field staff assisting with interview guide design, research setup, fieldwork, and validation may have shaped participant selection or interpretation, alongside the absence of objective physical water-point outcome data.

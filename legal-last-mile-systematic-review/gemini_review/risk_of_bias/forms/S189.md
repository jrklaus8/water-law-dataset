## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Boisson et al. (2014) report a mixed-methods process evaluation embedded within a 100-village cluster-randomised controlled trial (50 intervention, 50 control villages) of India's Total Sanitation Campaign (TSC) in Puri district, Orissa. Appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018; no numeric composite score computed), the study demonstrates strong mixed-methods rationale and integration (5.1–5.4 = Yes), objective longitudinal physical inspection of household latrine construction across six field rounds, random Stata-generated 10% sampling of village household and VWSC lists, and cluster-level statistical comparisons respecting village randomization. However, overall methodological quality cannot exceed its weakest components and warrants moderate-to-high concern: (1) the qualitative component reports no coding or thematic analysis methods and zero verbatim quotations (1.2–1.5 = Can't tell); (2) enumerators could not be blinded (2.4 = No) and relied partly on incomplete NGO motivator logbooks with missing data across 2–13 villages; (3) survey non-response rates are unreported and joint-family boundaries were strategically misreported by households (2.3 = Can't tell); (4) intervention fidelity and uptake fell far short of the 90% target (51% completed latrines, 66% including unfinished/damaged; 2.5 = No), while actual latrine use was not measured; and (5) within-intervention associations between mobilisation awareness and coverage (Table 4) rely on unadjusted bivariate village-level linear regressions vulnerable to ecological confounding.

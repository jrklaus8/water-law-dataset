## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Overall judgment: Some concern (JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Cohort Studies; no numeric score computed). Zhou and Liang (2021) analyze a 7-year longitudinal panel of 290 Chinese prefecture-level cities (2008-2014) from national statistical yearbooks using two-way (city and year) fixed-effects regressions, city-clustered standard errors, lagged predictor models, and 2008-2014 change regressions to test whether cities with a higher share of temporary residents provide lower per-capita public green space, wastewater treatment, and solid waste treatment infrastructure. Methodological strengths include nationwide administrative coverage, restriction to pre-2014 to avoid confounding from the 2014 State Council hukou reform, two-way fixed effects absorbing time-invariant city heterogeneity and national macro shocks, and transparent reporting of null results for green space and 3-year lags. However, substantial methodological concerns temper causal and environmental-justice inferences: (1) hukou institutional exclusion is not directly measured by local legal rules, but proxied by public-security-registered >1-year temporary residents (omitting unregistered and short-term rural migrants); (2) prefecture-wide outcomes cannot verify intra-city service access in migrant neighborhoods; (3) total urban population (permanent + temporary) appears in the denominator of both the focal exposure and all per-capita outcomes, risking mechanical short-run denominator dilution when migrant populations surge (consistent with the null 3-year lag estimates); and (4) 13%-22% of city-years (and 7-14 cities) are missing across regression models without explanation or missing-data handling.

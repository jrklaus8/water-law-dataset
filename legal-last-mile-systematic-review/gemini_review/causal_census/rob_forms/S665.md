## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Chenoweth (2004) presents a comparative historical-institutional four-country case study (England and Wales/Britain, Argentina, Côte d'Ivoire, and Israel/Palestinian territories) examining how public, privatized, and small-scale independent ownership structures in water supply and sanitation have evolved over time. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework as a structured non-validated judgment, the paper's chief strength lies in its rich contextual synthesis of institutional arrangements, contract structures, regulatory tensions, and the de facto role of informal/independent water providers alongside formal utilities (D10 strong; D2, D3, D5 adequate). However, methodological rigor for empirical or legal inference is weak overall: the paper contains no dedicated Methods section, relies entirely on secondary literature, utility self-reports (e.g., Aguas Argentinas 2001), and ministry webpages rather than primary statutes or concession contracts (D1, D7 weak), selects only countries that privatized and generated substantial published literature (D6, D11 weak), uses non-standardized outcomes across cases (D4 weak), and offers narrative chronology rather than credible causal identification or confounding control (D8, D9 weak).

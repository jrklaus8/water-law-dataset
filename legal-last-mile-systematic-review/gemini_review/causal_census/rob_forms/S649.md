## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Some concern. Study S649 conducts an ecological municipality-level observational regression analysis across Mexican municipalities (N = 2,372 and N = 2,342 out of 2,454 total municipalities) using official INEGI census and public finance data from 1999-2005 to examine whether indigenous population share predicts slower piped water network expansion and lower per capita federal transfers. Methodological strengths include near-complete national coverage, standardized census measurement of inside-dwelling piped water connections and indigenous language speakers, and multivariable fractional logit GLM and OLS specifications controlling for baseline 2000 water coverage, population density, formal income, international migration, presidential vote share, and state fixed effects. Key limitations driving moderate risk of bias ('Some concern') include: (1) unexplained exclusion of 82 to 112 municipalities across models, likely related to missing data or undisclosed handling of zero values under natural-log transformations; (2) proxy measurement error in key fiscal and economic variables, as federal transfers (Aportaciones Federales) aggregate health, education, and infrastructure funds rather than isolating water-specific transfers, and 1999 Economic Census remunerations omit agricultural and informal income; (3) absence of quasi-experimental causal identification, leaving residual municipality-level confounding (e.g. terrain/hydrology, intra-municipal settlement dispersion, local administrative capacity) and simultaneity between 2000-2005 transfers and 2005 water coverage; and (4) econometric weaknesses including entering an uninstrumented endogenous spatial lag directly into GLM/OLS and comparing mediation models across slightly different sample sizes.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This analytical cross-sectional geospatial study compiles state and county administrative datasets across California's San Joaquin Valley floor to examine how historical irrigation district formation eras and water-right seniority relate to modeled groundwater reliance and crop composition, and whether Disadvantaged Communities inside irrigation district boundaries (ID-DACs, n = 97) differ in environmental and drinking-water burdens from DACs outside irrigation districts (GDCs, n = 56). Strengths include comprehensive regional coverage across eight counties, transparent open data/code archiving (Dryad and GitHub), and grounding in official statutory and administrative registries (LAFCO, SWRCB eWRIMS, Land IQ, DWR DAC Mapping Tool, and OEHHA CalEnviroScreen 4.0). However, overall methodological risk of bias is rated High concern under the JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional Checklist due to three core limitations: (1) complete absence of confounding identification and adjustment in both district-era comparisons and bivariate ID-DAC vs. GDC Wilcoxon tests (despite GDCs having more than twice the mean population of ID-DACs and unmeasured differences in municipal incorporation and domestic water-utility service); (2) exposure and outcome measurement vulnerabilities, including imputing "no record" surface water deliveries as zero, modeling groundwater reliance indirectly from asynchronous periods (2001–2015 deliveries vs. 2016 crop evapotranspiration), joining census-tract CalEnviroScreen percentiles via DAC place centroids, and classifying non-irrigation-district DACs as groundwater-dependent after excluding municipal and domestic water agencies; and (3) unadjusted multiple bivariate testing alongside unspecified clustering methods in the main text.

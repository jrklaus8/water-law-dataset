## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Vijay and Ghosh (2018) present a pedagogical public-administration case study (published in Emerald Emerging Markets Case Studies with an educational-use disclaimer and no research Methods section) examining the Sabar Shouchagar ('Toilets for Everyone') campaign in Nadia District, West Bengal, India (2013-2015). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment), the paper's chief strength lies in its rich institutional and jurisdictional description (D2, D10 strong; D3 adequate): it traces how the District Magistrate's office coordinated national/state statutory and administrative programs (MGNREGA, NRLM, NBA, SSA, ICDS), aligned elected Gram Panchayats and the Zilla Sabadhipati despite competing political incentives (such as road-contract kickbacks), expanded local supply chains (from 9 to 107 women's SHG-run rural sanitary marts plus two mason training centers), and instituted household ODF declarations and Para Najardari neighborhood monitoring committees across 6,620 catchment areas. However, as empirical causal or evaluative evidence, the study has major methodological weaknesses (D1, D4-D9, D11, D13 weak; D12 not assessable): it purposively selects an award-winning single district with no comparator arm, omits any primary sampling, interview, or documentary methodology, cites zero statutory provisions or administrative orders, and relies on secondary summary figures (coverage rising from 60% to 97%; toilet usage rising from 57% to 99.8%) whose underlying survey protocols are unreported in the prose (and Exhibits 1-6 on PDF pp. 13-16 appear only as unextracted figure captions in the cached text) while social desirability and administrative reporting pressures remain unexamined.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This analytical cross-sectional study analyzes Afrobarometer Round 7 household survey data (N = 44,778 across 34 African countries, 2016-2018) using binary and ordered probit models with country fixed effects and region-clustered standard errors to examine the association between regional utilities bribery and household access to clean water. Appraised with the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies, the study raises Some concern overall. Methodological strengths include large multi-country CAPI survey coverage, multivariable adjustment (individual bribery, area piped infrastructure, lived poverty, urbanicity, gender, regional non-utilities corruption, and country fixed effects), extensive specification checks (ordered probit, mixed-effects, leave-one-out regional incidence, and piped vs. non-piped stratification), and commendably transparent reporting—including an explicit disclaimer against causal claims and disclosure that earlier survey rounds (Rounds 5 and 6) show null or opposite-signed associations. Key sources of bias include exposure misclassification (the bribery question bundles water, sanitation, and electricity services together and codes non-seekers of services as zero), subjective self-reported outcome and enumerator infrastructure measures without objective water quality or continuity verification, potential reverse causality, and unmeasured subnational/regional confounders (e.g., regional economic capacity, climate, and utility governance).

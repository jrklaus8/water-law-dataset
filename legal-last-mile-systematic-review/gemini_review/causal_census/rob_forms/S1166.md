## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Jiang and Zheng (2014) conduct a 10-year longitudinal panel evaluation (1998-2007) of private sector participation (PSP) across 208 medium and large urban water utilities in 192 Chinese prefecture-level cities using NBS Annual Industrial Firm Surveys merged with China City Statistical Yearbooks. Appraised under ROBINS-I, the study has an overall Serious risk of bias, driven by two domains rated Serious (D1 Confounding and D5 Missing data) alongside Moderate risk in D2, D3, D4 and D6, and No information in D7. Specifically, although two-way fixed effects, 1-year lagged utility and city controls, Arellano-Bond dynamic panel estimators and an urban industrial profitability falsification test address time-invariant heterogeneity and observed prior profitability selection, PSP entry and frequent private-sector exits (35 exit episodes) depend on unmeasured time-varying local factors—including municipal leadership changes, investor networks and unobserved local government guarantees on purchase volumes or tariffs—creating serious residual confounding risk. Furthermore, while firm financial regressions cover ~1,500-2,072 utility-years (with 2004 employment completely missing), city-level domestic water supply and domestic user regressions drop to N = 814 (>53% missingness) with no missing-data diagnostics. Additional moderate concerns arise from misclassifying BOT/TOT/divestiture concessions as state-owned utilities, 27 three-year switching anomalies whose post-hoc recoding drives the statistical significance of profitability gains, and reliance on city-wide yearbook aggregates without direct utility-level measurements of water leakage, quality, pressure or retail tariffs.

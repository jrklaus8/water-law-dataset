## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Nzengya (2018) is a non-randomized post-intervention comparative cross-sectional evaluation of KIWASCO's Delegated Management Model (DMM) in two informal settlements in Kisumu, Kenya (Nyalenda A and Nyalenda B) compared against an adjacent non-DMM 'control' slum (Manyatta B), combining customer-intercept questionnaires (n = 294), structured kiosk observations, and membrane-filtration microbiological water testing (n = 46 samples, including 22 household storage samples). Appraised with ROBINS-I, the study has an overall Serious risk of bias. Classification of settlement-level DMM status is Low risk (D3), and laboratory enumeration of E. coli and total coliforms used a valid standard assay, though unblinded interviewer-administered recall items for monthly water expenditure and 6-month reliability introduce Moderate measurement risk (D6) and co-occurring French donor infrastructure investments create Moderate co-intervention concerns (D4). However, internal validity is compromised by Serious confounding risk (D1: post-only comparison of two donor-targeted treated slums against one control slum using unadjusted one-way ANOVAs with no pre-intervention baseline or covariate/cluster controls), Serious participant-selection risk (D2: intercepting adult customers at active individual kiosks post-intervention conditions on kiosk patronage, excludes ~5% community-group kiosks and non-kiosk households, and exhibits inconsistent kiosk sample totals of 90 stated vs 97 summed vs 103 in ANOVA), No information on recruitment refusal rates (D5), and Serious selective/incomplete reporting (D7: pooling microbiological results across DMM and control slums in Table 1 and omitting comparative household water quantity figures despite claiming comparative evaluation on both dimensions).

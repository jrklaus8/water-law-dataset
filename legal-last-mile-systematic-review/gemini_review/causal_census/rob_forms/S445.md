## 2. Cross-Tool Summary Domains (`CODEBOOK.md` §8)

| `selection_bias` | High concern — Although drawn from the nationwide Inep School Census covering 3,424 municipalities, inclusion was conditioned on survival: only rural public elementary schools that remained active and linked to the same municipality in both 2011 and 2023 were retained (PDF p. 3). Because 22,138 rural schools closed or ceased offering regular elementary education over this period, survivor selection bias likely understates historical WASH deprivation among the smallest and most vulnerable closed schools. |
| `measurement_bias` | Moderate to high concern — School-level WASH and infrastructure data in the Inep Censo Escolar are self-declared by school principals without systematic external verification ('autodeclarados pelos diretores escolares e não passem por auditoria sistemática', PDF p. 3), with Inep acknowledging major questionnaire errors in other waves (2007-2010, 2017). Additionally, municipal IDHM-income from 2010 was reused for the 2023 cross-section because updated post-2022 IDHM data were unavailable (PDF p. 3), and category 8 ('povos e comunidades tradicionais') existed only in 2023 (PDF p. 5). |
| `confounding` | High concern — Multivariable binomial logistic regressions adjust for macroregion, differentiated territorial status, enrollment size, municipal population brackets, rural population percentage and municipal IDHM-income (PDF pp. 5, 8-10), but omit school administrative dependency (state vs. municipal networks) and municipal water/sewerage utility coverage, fail to account for municipal-level clustering, and include endogenous/mutually dependent student yield variables (approval, failure and dropout rates) on the right-hand side as determinants of WASH absence, introducing reverse-causality and collider bias. |
| `attrition` | High concern — Between 2011 and 2023, the national universe of rural public elementary schools contracted from 56,544 to 34,406 (a net reduction of 22,138 schools, or 39.2%; PDF p. 3). Restricting the analytic dataset exclusively to schools that remained active and linked to the same municipality in both 2011 and 2023 excludes closed or paralyzed schools without comparing their baseline WASH deprivation against surviving schools. |
| `reporting_bias` | High concern — No study protocol or pre-analysis plan is reported, and full multivariable logistic regression tables are omitted from the article. The Results section selectively reports prose odds ratios and p-values for highlighted categories (PDF pp. 9-10) without 95% confidence intervals, standard errors, exact regression sample sizes, or model fit/collinearity diagnostics. |
| `legal_measurement_quality` | Moderate concern (adequate administrative coding of territorial designations; no direct program exposure measurement) — Legal-territorial school categories ('Localização diferenciada': settlement areas, Indigenous lands, quilombola communities, and traditional peoples/communities added in 2023) and rural public elementary school status under Lei nº 9.394/1996 (excluding EJA) are coded directly from official Inep School Census registries (Quadro 1, PDF p. 5). | `outcome_measurement_quality` | High concern — School WASH outcomes are restricted to three crude, unverified self-reported binary indicators in the Censo Escolar: complete absence of water supply, complete absence of sanitation/sewerage, and existence of any toilet (Quadro 1, PDF pp. 5-6). These proxies do not capture drinking-water potability or safety, supply continuity, toilet functionality/privacy, handwashing stations with soap, or menstrual hygiene facilities. |

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High concern (JBI narrative judgment; no numeric score). Corrêa et al. (2026) conduct a nationwide comparative analytical cross-sectional study of WASH absence in Brazilian rural public elementary schools in 2011 and 2023 using Inep School Census and IBGE municipal census data across 3,424 municipalities. Strengths include comprehensive national territorial coverage, transparent variable definitions (Quadro 1), and proactive technical consultation with Inep to avoid known historical questionnaire-error waves (2007-2010 and 2017). Large descriptive disparities—particularly acute WASH deprivation in the North region, on Indigenous lands, and in micro-schools with up to 10 students—are clearly documented in Tables 1 and 2. However, substantial methodological risks affect internal validity, especially for the multivariable regression claims: (1) restricting the sample to schools active in the same municipality in both 2011 and 2023 amid a 39.2% contraction of rural public elementary schools (22,138 closures) introduces severe survivor bias; (2) WASH outcomes rely on unverified principal self-declarations of crude binary absence/presence without potability, functionality, handwashing or menstrual-hygiene measures; (3) single-level binomial logistic regressions combine school- and municipal-level covariates without accounting for clustering within 3,424 municipalities and include endogenous, collinear student flow outcomes (approval, failure and dropout rates) as 'determinants' of WASH absence; and (4) full regression tables and 95% confidence intervals are omitted in favor of selective prose odds ratios and p-values.

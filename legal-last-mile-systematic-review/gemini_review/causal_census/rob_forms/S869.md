## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Silvestre et al. (2022) conduct a comparative panel analysis (2013-2020; N = 1,157 municipality-year observations from IBGE, FINBRA, and SNIS) evaluating the relationship between municipal participation in formal regional sanitation consortia (vs. stand-alone non-cooperation) and municipal sanitation expenditure, water service coverage, and sewage service coverage in Brazil. Appraised with ROBINS-I as a non-randomized comparative panel evaluation of an institutional governance arrangement, the study is at Serious overall risk of bias. Three domains are rated Serious: (1) confounding (D1), because municipalities self-select into consortia via city council vote specifically in response to low baseline coverage and fiscal stress, no quasi-experimental identification strategy is employed, municipality fixed effects are not absorbed in the 11-df between-subjects SPSS GLM despite 'fixed effects' terminology, key fiscal/socioeconomic confounders are omitted, and contemporaneous water/sewage investments (post-treatment mediators) are conditioned on; (2) participant selection (D2) and (3) missing data (D5), because restricting to matched, log-transformed positive water and sewage investment observations collapses roughly 5,570 Brazilian municipalities over eight years to just 1,157 complete observations without diagnostics on excluded municipalities or distinction between incident and prevalent consortium members. Classification of interventions (D3), deviations from intended interventions (D4), measurement of outcomes (D6), and selection of the reported result (D7—no pre-registration plus internal table/text inconsistencies including a blank Table 1 and contradictory coverage conclusions in Section 5) are each rated Moderate risk of bias.

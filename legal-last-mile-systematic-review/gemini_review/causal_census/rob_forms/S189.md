## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Boisson et al. (2014) report a mixed-methods process evaluation embedded within a 100-village cluster-randomised controlled trial of rural sanitation implemented under India's Total Sanitation Campaign (TSC) in Puri district, Orissa. Appraised with the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018; Screening S1-S2, Qualitative 1.1-1.5, Quantitative descriptive 4.1-4.5, and Mixed-Methods integration 5.1-5.5), the study demonstrates strong mixed-methods rationale, design integration, and triangulation (5.1-5.4 all 'Yes'), as well as rigorous longitudinal physical inspection of latrine construction by independent enumerators and valid cluster-level t-test comparisons of awareness across the 50 intervention and 50 control villages. However, per MMAT 2018 principles that overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component, moderate methodological concerns arise (5.5 rated 'No'): the qualitative component provides no description of qualitative coding or analytic procedures, no verbatim quotes, unspecified sampling for community interviews, and incomplete NGO motivator logbooks (1.2, 1.3, 1.5 'Can't tell'); the quantitative surveys do not report non-response or refusal rates (4.2, 4.4 'Can't tell'); village-level linear regressions in Table 4 are unadjusted for confounders with minor text-table reporting discrepancies; household denominators were affected by strategic joint-family reporting around subsidies; and actual behavioural latrine use (as opposed to construction) was not measured.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Wutich (2009) is a well-integrated two-stage mixed-methods study combining extended residential ethnography (2003–2004, with 2008 follow-up) and a 5-wave, 10-month longitudinal household panel survey of 72 randomly sampled households (75% response rate from 96 contacted out of 415 mapped residential structures) in Villa Israel, a peri-urban squatter settlement in southern Cochabamba, Bolivia. Methodological strengths include prolonged multilingual fieldwork (Spanish, Quechua, Aymara), random household sampling within the settlement, explicit operationalization of customary and contractual housing-tenure rules (landownership, proxy occupancy, anticrético, and formal exclusion of renters), use of a grounded 9-point Guttman water-insecurity scale, and candid triangulation that uncovers survey underreporting of conflict and separates political corruption shocks from seasonal scarcity effects. Key risks of bias center on the quantitative non-randomized panel component: (1) roughly 19–21% missing longitudinal data in repeated-measures analyses (N=57–58 vs. 72 enrolled) without attrition diagnostics, alongside qualitative proof of scarcity-induced out-migration by vulnerable renters; (2) unadjusted bivariate/ANOVA tests with no statistical control for household size, income, or physical access to a working tapstand, plus political confounding of Neighborhood Council attendance; (3) reliance on 1-week binary retrospective self-reports for tapstand use and Council participation (with an unexplained 120.0% row sum in Table 5's Dry Season row); and (4) post-hoc selection of the household respondent in 33% of households and unsystematic recording of rule violations. Per MMAT 2018 guidance, no composite numeric score is calculated; overall methodological concern is judged moderate, bounded by the limitations of the quantitative panel component.

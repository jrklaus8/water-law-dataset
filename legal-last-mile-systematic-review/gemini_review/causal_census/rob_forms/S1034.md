## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Granados and Sanchez (2014) conduct a municipal-level panel difference-in-differences (DiD) evaluation of Colombia's Law 142 of 1994 public utility reforms (1990-2004 for child mortality; 1993-2005 censuses for water and sewerage coverage), augmented by propensity-score common-support trimming in the mortality models. Evaluated with ROBINS-I, the study has an overall Serious risk of bias. Its principal strengths are clear grounding in Colombian statutory provisions (Law 142 of 1994, Law 60 of 1993, Law 286 of 1996, Law 715 of 2001), administrative SSPD coding of provider corporate form and subscriber scale, two-way fixed effects with time-varying controls (including subsidized poor health insurance) in the mortality panel, and Propensity Score trimming based on 1994 municipal characteristics. However, three serious threats to internal validity remain: (1) confounding from pronounced differential pre-1994 child mortality trends visible in Figures 3-4 (untested for parallel pre-trends and unadjusted for municipality-specific trends) plus omitted time-varying covariates in the coverage DiD models (Table 5); (2) selection bias from excluding 329 municipalities with authorized rural/community organizations and, critically, truncating the infectious-disease child mortality regressions strictly to nonzero death records (dropping over half of observations based on the realized outcome); and (3) substantial unexplained panel missingness across Tables 3-5 alongside acknowledged vital registration underreporting in DANE mortality data (Note 14).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This PhD dissertation comprises three observational econometric essays examining drinking water regulation, reliability, and utility pricing in the United States and West Virginia, appraised under the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies with an overall judgment of Some concern (note: PDF pages 1-102 of 180 were accessible, covering the Abstract, Chapter 1, complete Essay 1, and nearly all of Essay 2's main text, whereas Essay 2's tables/appendices and Essay 3's full chapter on pp. 103-180 were truncated). Across the readable chapters, inclusion criteria, settings, administrative outcome measures (CMS real per capita health care spending, County Assessor arm's-length home sale prices, and PSD residential water tariffs), and multivariable/spatial confounding controls are clearly specified (Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7 = Yes). However, key methodological weaknesses warrant caution: (1) substantial exposure measurement error and misclassification (Q3 = No), including ~40% state non-reporting of Safe Drinking Water Act health-based violations to EPA SDWIS and coarse state-year ecological aggregation in Essay 1, plus exclusion of 90 of 440 Marion County Boil Water Notices (20.5%) due to missing street locations or lift dates in Essay 2 (causing affected homes during those episodes to be coded as unexposed); (2) absence of a quasi-experimental causal identification strategy across all three essays; and (3) statistical specification concerns in Essay 1 (Q8 = Unclear), where non-spatial state-plus-year fixed-effects models yield a null effect (0.000, p = 0.910), the Hausman test favors Fixed Effects, yet primary conclusions rely on a Random Effects Spatial Durbin Model alongside truncated statistical documentation for Essay 3.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Mwaura et al. (2021) evaluate the impact of household membership in Water Resource Users Associations (WRUAs) / Community Water Development Projects (CWDPs) on four binary poverty outcomes (consumption poverty, income poverty, subjective Economic Ladder poverty, and polychoric-PCA water poverty) using a 2019 cross-sectional survey of 652 rural smallholder households (331 WRUA members and 321 non-members) in Kenya's Upper Ewaso Ng'iro North Catchment Area. Because the study estimates causal treatment effects (ATT and ATU) of an institutional water-governance intervention against a non-member comparator using Full Information Maximum Likelihood Endogenous Switching Probit (ESP) with exclusion restrictions alongside Propensity Score Matching (PSM) and Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW), ROBINS-I is the appropriate appraisal instrument. Overall risk of bias is rated Serious, driven by Serious concerns in confounding (D1), participant selection (D2), outcome measurement (D6), and selective/inconsistent result reporting (D7), Moderate concern in intervention classification (D3), and No information on intervention deviations/spillovers (D4) and missing data (D5). Key threats include prevalent-user cross-sectional sampling drawn from WRUA lists without baseline pre-treatment data or sampling weights; conditioning on post-treatment covariates; invalid exclusion restrictions (notably 'Water conflict', which is mechanically embedded inside the Water Poverty Index in Table A2 and exhibits an unstarred z = 4.44 association with non-member water poverty in Table A1 Model 5); mechanical contamination of the Water Poverty Index with right-hand-side socioeconomic covariates; and reporting discrepancies such as a positive IPW ATT (+0.091**) for subjective poverty in Table 7 narrated in the text as a 9% poverty reduction.

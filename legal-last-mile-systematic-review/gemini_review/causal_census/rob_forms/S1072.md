## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This cross-national unbalanced panel study evaluates the effect of International Monetary Fund (IMF) structural adjustment lending on infant mortality across Sub-Saharan Africa in 1990, 1995, 2000, and 2005 (N = 70 country-year observations) using two-way fixed effects OLS and two-way fixed effects instrumental-variable (IV) regressions, appraised under ROBINS-I as having Serious overall risk of bias. While country and period fixed effects account for time-invariant national heterogeneity and common regional shocks, four ROBINS-I domains are rated at Serious risk of bias: (1) Confounding (D1): critical time-varying confounders such as armed conflicts, droughts, and acute macroeconomic crises are omitted, exposure and mortality are measured contemporaneously without lags, and the IV replication completely omits the identity of the excluded instruments, first-stage F-statistics, and exclusion-restriction justification; (2) Selection (D2) and (3) Missing data (D5): complete-case restriction leaves only 70 country-years (average 2.3 of 4 waves) across an inconsistently reported 30 (Abstract), 32 (Sample text), or 31 (Tables 1-2) countries out of ~48 Sub-Saharan African states, without missing-data diagnostics; and (4) Selective reporting / analytical transparency (D7): descriptive statistics and all first-stage IV results are withheld, one-tailed tests (including p < 0.10) are used throughout, text-table discrepancies appear (including claiming IMF lending is significant in every equation despite null results in Equations 1.9, 2.10, 2.5, and 2.6), and partial mediation via 'access to an improved water and sanitation source' is inferred solely from minor IMF coefficient attenuation without ever estimating the effect of IMF lending on water/sanitation access (which is moreover non-significant in the IV models). Legal/policy measurement is coarse (logged annual per-capita IMF loan disbursements without conditionality coding), and water/sanitation is only included as a combined improved-access covariate rather than a directly modeled outcome.

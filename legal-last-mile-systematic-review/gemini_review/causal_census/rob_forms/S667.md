## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Gyau-Boakye and Ampomah (2003) present a single-country institutional and legal-historical case study ("information study") of water pricing policies and water sector governance reforms in Ghana from 1965 to 2002 across urban, rural, agricultural, and water-resources management sub-sectors. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured non-validated judgment with no numeric composite score), the study demonstrates strong legal source accuracy and jurisdictional/institutional specificity—accurately tracing statutory instruments (Acts 310, 522, and 538; SMCD 85; L.I. 1350 and L.I. 1597), tariff-approval mechanics, ministerial delays, public-sector arrears, and administrative reorganization (GWSC/GWCL, CWSD/CWSA, GIDA, PURC, and WRC). However, it has no formal Methods section, no primary sampling or document-selection protocol, weak measurement transparency (relying on grey agency/donor reports without audit details, though candidly noting settlement-base changes that invalidate 1992 vs. 1998/1999 coverage comparisons), no counterfactual causal identification, weak separation of pricing/legal reforms from US$240 million in concurrent donor rehabilitation and debt relief, and no reflexive discussion of the second author's insider role at the Water Resources Commission. Consequently, it provides valuable descriptive legal-institutional context but carries high risk of bias if interpreted as causal evidence on the impacts of water pricing or regulatory reform.

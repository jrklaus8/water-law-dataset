## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Appraised using the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies, S407 raises 'Some concern' overall (with higher concern if its econometric associations are interpreted causally rather than descriptively). On the strength side, the paper clearly defines respondent and spatial-unit inclusion criteria (Q1: Yes), thoroughly characterizes Nigeria's administrative tiers, ethnic geography, and survey cohorts (Q2: Yes), uses objective cartographic and administrative boundaries validated against independent ethnicity datasets (Q4: Yes), and identifies and controls for numerous geographic, ecological, and historical slave-trade confounders in its primary spatial OLS models (Q5: Yes; Q6: Yes). Methodological concerns center on construct validity of the legal/institutional exposures (Q3: No), outcome aggregation and measurement assumptions (Q7: Unclear), and analytical limitations in the mechanism tests (Q8: Unclear). Specifically, elite noncompliance with the 1976 Local Government Reform Law is never directly observed but proxied by interacting Murdock's c. 1850 centralization indicator with a 1952 state-level >70% Muslim dummy; water and sanitation are globally labelled 'locally administered' and flush toilets 'federally administered' without measuring state water utility governance or household self-supply; spatial coverage drops from 1,415 LGA-ethnic partitions to 631 complete cases (and 135-160 LGAs in the 2012 Afrobarometer regressions); and Table 7 regresses 2013 DHS outcomes on 2012 subjective local-government perceptions controlling only for population density, leaving reverse causality and omitted confounders unaddressed. The author appropriately caveats that the OLS estimates represent correlations rather than definitive causal proof.

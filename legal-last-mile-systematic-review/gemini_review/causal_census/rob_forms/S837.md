## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Overall judgment: Some concern. Scott, Moldogaziev, and Greer (2018) conduct a well-structured longitudinal observational panel (cohort) study of 527 public special purpose residential water districts (MUDs, FWSDs, and WCIDs) in the Houston MSA from 2008 to 2016, linking EPA SDWIS violation histories (2005-2016), TCEQ/TWDD audited annual finances, GIS-weighted ACS median home values, and Texas Bond Review Board new tax-exempt bond issuances via a two-part semi-continuous hierarchical Bayesian binomial-gamma model. Methodological strengths include comprehensive institutional coverage of active Houston special water districts, clear legal separation of substantive SDWA health violations from procedural management violations, careful bond-purpose filtering, appropriate multilevel two-part Bayesian estimation, and multiple specification checks (8 of 11 JBI Cohort items rated 'Yes'). However, three JBI Cohort items are rated 'No' (Q6, Q9, Q10) and support an overall judgment of Some concern: (1) sample entry was conditioned on districts active in 2016, introducing survivorship bias if distressed districts consolidated or dissolved earlier, while panel completeness was low (only 7 of 527 districts had all nine annual audits from 2008 to 2016, and 78 had five or fewer) with no exploration of missingness mechanisms or formal missing-data adjustment beyond ad hoc last-observation-carried-forward for zero customer counts; (2) debt issuance is a repeatable event in districts carrying substantial baseline debt, and a 3-year lagged violation window permits corrective borrowing earlier in the window prior to year t; and (3) equating capital investment with bond issuance omits cash-financed pay-as-you-go infrastructure spending, SDWA violations depend on utility self-monitoring, and observational multilevel models remain vulnerable to unobserved confounding such as rate structures and baseline physical asset decay.

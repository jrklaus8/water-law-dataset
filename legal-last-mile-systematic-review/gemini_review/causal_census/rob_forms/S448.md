## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Study S448 (Ribeiro et al., 2026) is a non-randomized comparative longitudinal panel study evaluating the association between intermunicipal cooperation (IMC via public consortia) and municipal water and wastewater service (WWS) performance in Brazil over 2015-2019 (344 direct-provision municipalities; N = 1,720). Appraised with ROBINS-I, the overall risk of bias is Serious. Methodological strengths include the use of two-way fixed effects (municipality and year fixed effects) alongside random-effects and Mundlak correlated random-effects specifications, municipality-clustered standard errors, and standardized national datasets (IBGE MUNIC and SNIS) across four performance dimensions (financial balance, water coverage, wastewater coverage, and inverted water-loss quality). However, three domains carry Serious risk of bias: (1) Confounding (D1), as voluntary consortium participation and linear cooperation duration (IMCd) are vulnerable to unmeasured time-varying municipal fiscal, political, and investment shocks and differential pre-trends among the 54 already-treated municipalities (only lnPOP and year dummies are controlled); (2) Selection of participants (D2), due to prevalent-user/survivorship bias (54 of 71 IMC municipalities entered cooperation prior to 2015, with only 17 switchers during 2015-2019) and conditioning on uninterrupted 5-year reporting; and (3) Missing data (D5), as complete-case balanced-panel construction excludes 76.6% of Brazilian municipalities that directly provide WWS without missingness diagnostics or reweighting. Classification of interventions (D3) and outcome measurement (D6) present Moderate risk due to coarse consortium coding and self-reported SNIS metering/accounting proxies, Deviations from intended interventions (D4) is Low, and Selection of the reported result (D7) is rated No information in the absence of a pre-analysis plan.

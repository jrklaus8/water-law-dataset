## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment; no numeric composite score), Pierce (2012) is a single-city political-economy and institutional-historical case study of water service privatization contracts in Mexico City (Distrito Federal, 1994-2011), supplemented by unadjusted aggregate INEGI census tabulations (1990, 2005, 2010) and three informal stakeholder communications. Methodological strengths lie in jurisdictional specificity (D2: strong) and multi-scalar institutional contextualization (D10: strong): the author carefully distinguishes the DF's 16 boroughs and 4 concession zones from surrounding State of Mexico metropolitan rings (Outer ZMCM and Outer ZMVM) while holding pre-2004 SEDESOL boundaries constant, and traces municipal-federal partisan dynamics (PRI, PRD, PAN), consortium restructurings, and COMDA's invocation of the 2002 Federal Transparency Law. Legal source accuracy (D1), exposure definition (D3), outcome definition (D4), and measurement transparency (D7) are adequate, though constrained by reliance on secondary literature and press reports rather than primary concession contracts or statutory texts (compounded by SACM's post-2003 secrecy). Conversely, sampling transparency (D5), source/informant selection (D6), causal identification (D8), treatment of confounding (D9), replication potential (D11), coding transparency (D12), and researcher reflexivity (D13) are weak. In particular, Table 3 compares aggregate connection rates across zones experiencing vastly different 20-year population growth (5% in DF vs. 52% in Outer ZMCM and 57% in Outer ZMVM) without statistical adjustment, and the Conclusions overstate causal attribution regarding efficiency and infrastructure improvements despite the text's earlier admission on p. 682 that causal attribution to privatization is unclear.

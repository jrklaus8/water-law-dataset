## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Hailu, Osorio, and Tsukada (2012) conduct a repeated cross-sectional comparative evaluation and difference-in-differences (DiD) probit analysis of water utility privatization in Bolivia (1997-2005 concession in La Paz and El Alto vs. cooperative provision in Santa Cruz de la Sierra and public provision in Cochabamba) using four INE household survey waves (1992, 1996, 2001, 2005), contextualized with secondary regulatory and audit documentation. Appraised with ROBINS-I, the study has a Serious overall risk of bias. Methodological strengths include distributional disaggregation by income quintile, concentration curves/indices, Kakwani achievement indices accounting for baseline coverage ceilings, low item missingness in the water-access regressions, and candid acknowledgment of causal caveats and contractual non-compliance (SISAB's 2005 audit). However, causal inference is seriously constrained by: (D1) non-random policy assignment across only four cities with divergent 1992-1996 pre-trends, limited household covariates due to survey incompatibility, unadjusted time-varying city shocks, and unclustered standard errors; (D2) unspecified INE survey sampling designs, severe sample shrinkage in El Alto in 2001 (n = 56), and conditioning the affordability analysis on post-intervention connection status; and (D6) shifts in survey water-source categories between 1992/1996 and 2001/2005 (Table 4) plus lack of volumetric water consumption data or 1996 baseline expenditure data. Moderate concerns also arise from classifying Cochabamba as a non-privatized control despite its turbulent 1999-2000 privatization (D3/D4), and no pre-analysis plan exists (D7: No information).

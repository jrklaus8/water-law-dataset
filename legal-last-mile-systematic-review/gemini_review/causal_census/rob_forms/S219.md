## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Aquino and Ledo (2023) combine a stylized two-period game-theoretic model of regulatory price-setting under electoral incentives (Section 3) with an unbalanced municipal/utility panel econometric analysis across 88 ANA-recognized subnational sanitation regulatory agencies in Brazil from 2002 to 2021 (Section 4; N = 1,142 total observations, N = 983 in regressions). Appraised with ROBINS-I for its comparative longitudinal panel evaluation of municipal and state election years on real regulated tariff adjustments (Variação_real), the empirical component is at Serious overall risk of bias. First, there is serious selection bias (D2): utilities enter the panel at their oldest located online adjustment resolution, and municipalities that enacted zero tariff adjustments over the entire 2002–2021 period—which the authors explicitly note may reflect the most severe political interference—are completely excluded. Second, there is serious confounding risk (D1): because Brazilian municipal and state elections occur on fixed nationwide 4-year calendars across all municipalities simultaneously and the regressions omit general calendar-year fixed effects, nationwide macroeconomic and IPCA inflation shocks in election years are not separated from electoral cycle effects. Third, missing data and outcome measurement carry serious risk of bias (D5, D6): ~14% of observations are dropped in regressions due to missing SNIS sewage coverage, post-entry resolutions were not found for all years, and multi-year gaps where contacted agencies did not reply were imputed as 0% nominal adjustments (automatically generating negative real tariff changes). Finally, empirical reporting exhibits multiple editorial/technical errors—including a misstated dummy definition (p. 135), a negative maximum (-0,661) in Table 1 (p. 134), duplicated row labels and omitted control estimates in Table 3 (p. 136), a contradictory Hausman test explanation (p. 136), and no direct empirical test of physical network expansion as an outcome.

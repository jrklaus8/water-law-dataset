## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Gaikwad and Thomas (2026) report a large-scale 2x2 multi-level cluster-randomized controlled trial across 76 informal settlements (152 analyzed slum clusters; N=6,652 endline households) in Mumbai evaluating whether household-level NGO bureaucratic facilitation (T1, cluster-randomized) and community-level political coordination/mobilization (T2, block-randomized at the slum level) improve formalized municipal piped water access, supplemented by 9 illustrative stakeholder interviews in Appendix H. Appraised with the RoB 2 cluster-randomized trials variant, the study has an overall rating of 'Some concerns'. Key methodological strengths include rigorous two-stage covariate-blocked randomization with verified baseline balance (D1a: Low), high-quality legal/institutional coding of court orders, municipal water circulars, and land-authority NOC rules, and objective endline outcome verification via physical enumerator inspection of household BMC pipes and attached water meters four years post-intervention (D4: Low). However, four domains raise 'Some concerns': (D1b) recruitment of 2,444 new 'replacement sample' households at the 2023 endline (~36.7% of analyzed observations) after unblinded cluster treatments had unfolded, without reporting an original-panel-only sensitivity analysis; (D2) high background NGO activity in control clusters (39%) and potential intra-slum spillover given multi-cluster slums and post-evaluation NGO commitments elsewhere in treated settlements; (D3) 38.5% attrition of original baseline respondents over five years due to slum demolitions and migration (though aggregate attrition rates were balanced across arms); and (D5) reliance on an 'eligible lands' subsample (excluding 28 slums on footpath, forest, and salt pan lands via a July 2019 post-implementation PAP amendment, plus a February 2024 post-endline-fieldwork PAP amendment) for the headline complementarity effect while full-sample and ordinal-scale primary interaction tests are null—mitigated by the authors' transparent side-by-side reporting of all full-sample null results.

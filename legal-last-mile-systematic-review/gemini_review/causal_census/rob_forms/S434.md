## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Study S434 (Frempong, Stadelmann, & Thiam, 2025) merges geo-coded repeated cross-sectional Afrobarometer household survey data (rounds 3-6, 2005-2015; N = 142,838 across 28 African countries) with geo-coded commercial mining locations from the World Bank Africa-PowerMining database (435 sites) and USGS mineral deposit records to estimate the effects of active and inactive mines (within 0-50 km and 50-100 km buffers) and local institutional quality on self-reported water insecurity, medicine deprivation, and income deprivation. Appraised with ROBINS-I as a non-randomized quasi-experimental (2SLS/IV) exposure evaluation, the study is judged at Serious overall risk of bias, driven by Serious concerns in confounding (D1) and exposure classification (D3), alongside Moderate concerns in participant selection (D2), co-occurring informal mining deviations (D4), and self-reported outcome measurement (D6), with No information on missing data (D5) and pre-analysis protocol registration (D7). Specifically, while mineral deposit instruments help address endogenous mine placement in Table 2 (Kleibergen-Paap F = 13.044), instrumentation becomes weak when jointly separating active and inactive mines in Table 3 (F = 7.716), key hydrological/topographic variables (river networks, elevation, rainfall) are omitted, potential post-treatment mediators (employment, piped water, clinics, electricity) are controlled for, and the local governance moderation regressions (Table 4) abandon IV entirely in favor of OLS/LPM using contemporaneous survey-aggregated corruption perceptions. Furthermore, the commercial mine inventory lacks reported time-varying opening/closure dates across 2005-2015 and omits widespread artisanal mining, while binary self-reported water scarcity cannot capture invisible chemical or heavy-metal water contamination.

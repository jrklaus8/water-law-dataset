## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Feler and Henderson (2008) conduct a non-randomized longitudinal/panel econometric evaluation of strategic municipal under-servicing of migrant housing in urban Brazil (1970-2000), combining spatial reaction-function models of 1991 central water provision to small (1-3 room) houses (Section 4) with urban-area fixed-effects 2SLS/LIML regressions of 1991-2000 locality household and educational growth on 1991 small-house water provision (Section 5). Appraised under ROBINS-I, the study is rated at Serious overall risk of bias, driven by Serious concerns in confounding (D1) and intervention classification (D3), alongside Moderate concerns in participant/unit selection (D2), post-baseline policy shifts (D4), missing data/unextracted tables (D5), outcome measurement (D6), and selective reporting in the absence of a pre-analysis plan (D7). Key strengths include harmonizing split municipalities into constant-boundary Minimum Comparable Areas (MCAs), controlling for metropolitan shocks via urban-area fixed effects, explicitly confronting reverse causality (rapid growth creating servicing backlogs) via geological/insolation and 1970 historical instruments, and testing 1991 large-house and 2000 post-democratization counterfactuals. However, exclusionary municipal policy is never directly measured from legal or administrative acts—instead proxied by census water-connection rates in small houses—while rival-locality policy instruments failed in Section 4 and the Section 5 IV strategy relies on geological and historical exclusion restrictions that face plausible direct pathways and modest multivariate first-stage strength (partial F ~ 13).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This cross-sectional observational study analyzes fiscal, demographic, political, organizational, and environmental correlates of monthly household basic drinking water costs (6,000 gal/month) across 2,119 contiguous US municipalities with populations >= 10,000 that own and operate their own drinking water utility. Methodological strengths include comprehensive coverage of medium-to-large US municipal water systems via the validated Municipal Drinking Water Database, integration of authoritative federal and academic administrative datasets, and multivariable OLS estimation incorporating state fixed effects, state-clustered robust standard errors, and regional/electoral sensitivity analyses. Overall methodological concern is judged as 'Some concern' under the JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional Studies checklist due to four limitations: (1) unreported coder training or inter-rater reliability verification for manual extraction and calculation of 2,161 utility rate schedules by nine coders, alongside an unresolved discrepancy between the Methods text (rates collected Nov 2021-May 2022) and Appendix Tables A1-A2 footnotes ('2017 cost of water'); (2) asynchronous measurement timing between MDWD/climate covariates (2000-2018 archive; 2017 moisture index) and 2021-2022 water tariffs; (3) omitted variable bias from unmeasured infrastructure age, treatment technology, and source water quality, plus non-generalizability to small (< 10,000 population), private, or fragmented water systems; and (4) minor internal equation and table reporting errors (e.g., significance-star mismatches in Table 3 and unflipped variable labels in Table A1).

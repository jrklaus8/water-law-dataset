## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This quantitative analytical cross-sectional study examines associations between municipal institutional capacity (financial, political, operational, and technical dimensions) and coverage rates of water supply, sewerage, and solid waste collection across municipalities in Minas Gerais, Brazil, using 2018 SNIS and 2017 IBGE secondary data. Appraised with the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies, the study raises high methodological concern. Key strengths include the use of standardized national administrative definitions (Table 1), clear descriptive and spatial characterization of the Minas Gerais context (Table 2, Figure 2), heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors, and candid reporting of counter-intuitive results and self-reporting limitations. However, internal and external validity are substantially weakened by: (1) severe, inadequately documented sample truncation—dropping from Minas Gerais's 853 municipalities to 574 via unspecified box-plot outlier removal and then to 391 complete cases in OLS regressions (over 54% loss), where SNIS non-reporting is itself endogenous to weak municipal capacity; (2) complete absence of confounding adjustment in the regression models (omitting municipal population size, GDP/income, urbanization, and service-provider type despite discussing them in the text); (3) reverse causality/simultaneity in cross-sectional associations (notably municipalities with low water coverage newly adopting sanitation plans); (4) reliance on unverified self-reported SNIS data and binary plan/policy dummies; and (5) applying Pearson-correlation PCA to binary indicators alongside continuous variables.

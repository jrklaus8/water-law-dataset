## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This study is an analytical cross-sectional observational study examining the universe of the 500 largest US community water systems in 2015 (serving ~140 million residents across 48 states and DC) using multivariable OLS regression. Methodological strengths include complete census coverage of the top 500 systems with zero missing observations, primary compilation of January 2015 drinking water rate schedules standardized to 60,000 gallons/year, an affordability metric aligned with revised EPA (2020) guidance (percent of lowest-quintile household income), and adjustment for hydrological, infrastructure-age, and demographic covariates. However, there is 'Some concern' overall regarding risk of bias: (1) legal and regulatory constructs are coarsely measured—121 cooperatives are merged with 321 municipal utilities as 'public', PUC oversight of public utilities is coded as a state-level dummy across 6 states where only some public utilities are regulated, and 'favorable private regulation' (such as DSIC and fair market value statutes) is captured solely by a two-state indicator for New Jersey and Pennsylvania, conflating statutory mechanisms with unobserved state effects; (2) the single-period cross-sectional design cannot rule out reverse causality or endogenous selection of high-cost, aging municipal systems into privatization; (3) utility service areas are matched approximately to city/county ACS geographies and scheduled rates do not reflect low-income bill assistance programs; and (4) OLS regressions omit state/county clustered standard errors for state-level policy variables, leave strong right-skewness in the affordability ratio unaddressed, withhold raw data, and suffer from missing minus signs in Tables 1 and 2.

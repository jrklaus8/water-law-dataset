## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Overall judgment: Some concern (7 Yes, 1 Unclear on the JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional Checklist). Subramanyam (2020) provides a transparent, nearly national municipal-level analysis (N = 3,547 statutory cities and towns nested in 21 Indian states) using harmonized 2001 and 2011 Census of India Houselisting and Town Directory data and an appropriate two-level random-intercept regression with state-clustered standard errors. Key strengths include comprehensive statutory coverage, clear variable operationalization, and candid discussion of institutional caveats. Methodological concerns center on: (1) proxy measurement and temporal ordering, as unobserved municipal tax bases and administrative capacities are proxied using 2011 endline city size and statutory local body classifications (and state covariates from 2004/2007) rather than strictly pre-period 2001 measures; (2) substantial potential for residual confounding and reverse causality in an observational cross-sectional change regression—illustrated by unmeasured city-level parastatal vs. municipal water governance arrangements and endogenous targeting of NURM/UIDSSMT grants; (3) exclusion of Union Territories, seven northeastern states, and non-statutory census towns, plus an unexplained 11-city discrepancy between Table 1–2 category counts (3,558) and the regression sample (3,547); and (4) reliance on nominal in-dwelling tap presence without measures of water potability, treatment, or continuity of supply.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This quantitative analytical cross-sectional household survey (N = 593 analysed of 600 randomly sampled households) and GIS infrastructure mapping study in Mandala, Mumbai, exhibits strong field execution and low missingness (1.2%), using complete census enumeration, two-week repeated measurement of household water consumption via a validated container-enumeration protocol and explicit separation of confounders (income, religion, household size) from infrastructural mediators. Overall methodological concern is rated as 'Some concern' under the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies (7 'Yes', 1 'Unclear'). The principal risk of bias arises in exposure measurement and cluster-level confounding (Q3 = Unclear): although statutory slum notification under Maharashtra law applies at the household level based on pre-2000 proof of residence, legal status was operationalized as a binary neighbourhood-level classification comparing a single notified neighbourhood (Matangrushi Nagar, near a major road and settled earlier) against a single non-notified neighbourhood (Indira Nagar, farther from the road near a river and landfill and settled later) based on community researchers' local boundary knowledge rather than verified household legal documentation. Consequently, household-level legal status may be misclassified for a subset of residents, and unobserved neighbourhood-level geographic or historical determinants cannot be statistically separated from legal status. Secondary concerns include respondent recall for weekly container fills and water expenditures, 12% 'unsure' responses on household income, non-differentiation between piped municipal taps and brackish borewell taps in survey questions, absence of microbiological water quality testing and lack of cluster-adjusted standard errors or GSEM goodness-of-fit diagnostics.

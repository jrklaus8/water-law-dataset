## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Overall methodological judgment: Some concern (JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional Studies checklist: 5 'Yes' [Q1, Q2, Q5, Q6, Q8], 3 'Unclear' [Q3, Q4, Q7], 0 'No'). Thomas-Possee et al. (2024) present a well-documented cross-sectional spatial linkage of nationally representative 2018 Zambia Demographic and Health Survey urban/peri-urban piped-water households (n = 3,047 in 177 clusters) with 2017 IBNET utility data and 2018 NWASCO statutory regulator data across all 11 commercial Water and Sewerage Companies. Strengths include transparent multi-stage country and household eligibility flowcharts, low item missingness, explicit identification of multi-scale confounders, multicollinearity diagnostics, and two-level logistic regression accounting for cluster random effects and provider fixed effects, alongside open data and code links. Methodological concerns center on measurement and selection limitations inherent to the secondary data sources: household intermittency relies on unvalidated 2-week recall of full-day outages (missing sub-24-hour outages and daytime outages when respondents are away); utility and regulator performance metrics rely on unverified provider self-reports with unclear operational definitions of service hours; rural piped-water users (16% of national piped users) and small-scale non-commercial schemes are excluded, compounded by mismatches between the 2010 DHS urban/rural sampling frame and 2015 GHSL settlement data; key coping confounders such as household storage tanks were unmeasured; and multilevel models omitted survey weights and used backward elimination (p = 0.05) while highlighting marginally significant (p < 0.1) associations.

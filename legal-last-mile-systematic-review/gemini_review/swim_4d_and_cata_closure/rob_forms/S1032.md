## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High concern. Russ and Takahashi (2013) present an analytical cross-sectional survey of 300 leaders and charter members across 38 community-based organisations (CBOs) in upgraded neighbourhoods of the Ahmedabad Slum Networking Project (SNP), examining correlates of overall programme satisfaction and open-ended complaints regarding water, sewerage, and street lighting. Strengths include clearly stated CBO/member eligibility criteria, rich contextual and demographic comparison against city-wide slum benchmarks, and thoughtful conceptual distinction between professed satisfaction and complaining behaviour. However, substantial methodological limitations warrant high concern: (1) survivorship and elite selection bias, as sampling was restricted to formal CBO charter leaders/members in NGO-affiliated communities registered for at least three years that had already received physical upgrading (excluding failed/unregistered CBOs and ordinary non-member slum residents, and yielding a 70.7% female, 97.7% Hindu sample); (2) common-method and recall bias from measuring exposures and outcomes via unvalidated cross-sectional self-reports and unstandardized NGO ratings, alongside open-ended complaint coding without reported inter-rater reliability and zero objective physical measurements of water or sewerage service quality; and (3) flawed statistical modelling that ignores clustering of 300 individuals within 38 CBOs when testing CBO- and neighbourhood-level predictors, relies on bivariate significance pre-screening that drops core exposures and confounders inconsistently across models, and cites 2SLS instrumental-variable robustness checks without reporting instruments, diagnostics, or estimates.

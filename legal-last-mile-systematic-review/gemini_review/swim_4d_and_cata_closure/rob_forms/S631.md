## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This longitudinal quasi-experimental panel study evaluates the effect of Indonesian local government proliferation (pemekaran—administrative splitting of kabupaten/kota) between 2001 and 2010 on household access to protected water/sanitation and secondary school enrollments, collapsing newly created parent and child districts into all 336 original 2001 district boundaries and estimating static generalized difference-in-differences (with district fixed effects, year fixed effects, and district-specific linear trends) and dynamic Arellano-Bond difference-GMM models. Methodological strengths include comprehensive national coverage, careful institutional grounding in statutory vs. operational split dates, lead falsification tests (Table 6), and weak-instrument/overidentification diagnostics (Tables 5 and A3). Nevertheless, overall risk of bias under ROBINS-I is rated Serious, driven primarily by confounding (D1): (1) whereas the author justifies causal identification via exogenous timing of splits among ever-splitting districts (Table A2), comparing newly operational districts strictly against not-yet-split districts (Split vs. New, 98 groups) yields no statistically significant effect on infrastructure access, and the reported ~1.0 to 1.35 percentage-point decline emerges only when including 238 never-split districts that had ~10 percentage points higher initial service access (Original vs. New); (2) the models condition on post-treatment district total expenditure per capita, which rises sharply after splitting; and (3) concurrent time-varying institutional changes such as the 2005-2010 rollout of direct local executive elections are unadjusted. Moderate concerns also arise from ~10-15% unexplained missing district-years (D5), undocumented weighting when collapsing split-district survey percentages and composite averaging of self-reported water and sanitation access without quality measures (D6), and absence of disaggregated water vs. sanitation estimates or a pre-analysis plan (D7).

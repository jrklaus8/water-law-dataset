## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Using ROBINS-I, this quasi-experimental difference-in-differences and propensity-score-matching evaluation of Recife's Social Interest Special Zones (ZEIS) is judged at Serious overall risk of bias, driven by Domain 1 (confounding). Key methodological strengths include the exclusion of prevalent pre-1991 ZEIS tracts, exact geocoding of municipal ZEIS boundaries and enactment years onto a balanced citywide panel of harmonized IBGE Demographic Census tracts (1991, 2000, and 2010), isolation of the 1993-1996 treatment cohort to avoid staggered DiD weighting issues, and extensive sensitivity checks across ZEIS territory-share thresholds (50%-100%), slum-only comparators, and spillover-free neighborhoods. However, because pre-1991 census tracts could not be harmonized, only one pre-intervention wave (1991) is observed, rendering the parallel-trends assumption empirically untestable in a setting where ZEIS enactment required specific municipal legislation and mapping resources potentially linked to unobserved community mobilization. Conditioning on post-treatment tract covariates (density, literacy, marriage), ecological aggregation without longitudinal household tracking, and reliance on self-reported census ownership and sewerage indicators introduce additional moderate concerns across Domains 2, 3, 6, and 7.

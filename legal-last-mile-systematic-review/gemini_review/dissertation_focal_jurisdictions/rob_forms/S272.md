## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Almazán-Casali, Peregrina Puga, and Lemos (2021) present a well-contextualized primary qualitative case study examining why Brazil's decentralized, participatory Piracicaba-Capivari-Jundiaí (PCJ) River Basin Committees doubled down on grey-infrastructure solutions rather than adaptive or nature-based governance after the 2014–2015 São Paulo water crisis. Methodological strengths include clear research aims (Q1), an appropriate qualitative case design triangulating master water plans, news coverage, multi-year observation of committee and technical chamber meetings, and 27 semi-structured interviews across engineering and non-engineering committee actors (Q2–Q5), documented IRB approval and confidentiality protections (Q7), and balanced, transparent presentation of competing Ways of Knowing (Q9–Q10). Moderate methodological concern arises from two gaps out of Q1–Q9: the absence of researcher reflexivity regarding multi-year observer presence, academic gatekeeper introductions, and normative theoretical framing (Q6: No), and incomplete reporting of analytic rigor regarding coder independence/consensus in NVivo and Portuguese-to-English translation verification (Q8: Can't tell). Moreover, snowball recruitment among active committee participants leaves inactive or marginalized stakeholders outside the core deliberative arenas unexamined, and water/sanitation governance effects are assessed through institutional planning priorities and perceptions rather than physical service-delivery outcomes.

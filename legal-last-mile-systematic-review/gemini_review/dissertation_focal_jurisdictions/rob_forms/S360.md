## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Galway (2016) conducts an administrative cross-sectional descriptive and time-trend analysis of 402 drinking water advisories (DWAs) issued across First Nations community drinking water systems in Ontario, Canada, between 2004 and 2013, linking Health Canada DWA records to system attributes from the 2011 Ontario Regional Roll-Up Report (2009–2010 site visits). Appraised with the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross Sectional Studies, the study is rated High concern for analytical/associational inference (3 Yes, 3 Unclear, 2 No), though it serves usefully as a transparent descriptive baseline. Inclusion criteria (Q1) and community/system context (Q2) are well specified, and key potential confounders such as surveillance changes, hydroclimatic seasonality, and remoteness are thoughtfully discussed (Q5). However, system characteristics were measured only once in 2009–2010 with unverified error rates and applied across a 10-year window (Q3 = Unclear); DWA issuance and reporting on reserves are voluntary and precautionary rather than mandatory standardized water-quality outcomes (Q4, Q7 = Unclear); no statistical adjustment for confounding was conducted (Q6 = No); and statistical analyses relied on unadjusted Poisson counts using post-hoc minimum-count reference categories without accounting for within-system clustering or overdispersion, while tabulating system characteristics strictly among DWA events without non-DWA system denominators (Q8 = No). Minor arithmetic/typographical inconsistencies also appear in reported totals (e.g., Table 3 operator training count and total advisory days).

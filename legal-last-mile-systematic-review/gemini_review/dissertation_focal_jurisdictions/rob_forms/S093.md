## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Gonçalves, Paiva Júnior, and Cerqueira (2026) present a primary qualitative institutional case study of water governance in the São Francisco River Integration Project (PISF) in Pernambuco, Brazil, applying Elinor Ostrom's Institutional Analysis and Development (IAD) framework to documentary sources, field visits, and interviews conducted between 2015 and 2018. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits clear research aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative institutional methodology and IAD-guided case design (Q2: Yes; Q3: Yes), and a coherent thematic statement of findings on federal/state centralization, fragile access enforcement along canals crossing private properties, obsolete municipal distribution infrastructure, and exclusion of Indigenous and quilombola communities (Q9: Yes). However, moderate methodological concerns arise across five domains (1 No and 4 Can't tell items): researcher reflexivity and positionality are entirely unaddressed despite fieldwork with vulnerable traditional communities (Q6: No); total sample size, selection mechanics, and apparent non-inclusion of stated landowner/municipal actor categories are unclear (Q4: Can't tell); interview guides, durations, and saturation criteria are omitted (Q5: Can't tell); formal ethics committee (CEP/CONEP) approval is unreported beyond a brief note on recording consent (Q7: Can't tell); and although NVivo 11 pragmatic language analysis is cited, zero verbatim interview quotations or coding matrices are provided in the Results to separate primary interview evidence from secondary documents (Q8: Can't tell).

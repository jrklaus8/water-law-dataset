## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Wang et al. (2025) conduct a global secondary burden-of-disease simulation, time-series forecasting, and demographic decomposition study estimating Deaths Attributable to Unsafe Sanitation in Rural areas (DAUSRs) across 154 countries from 2000 to 2030. Because the study combines deterministic Comparative Risk Assessment (CRA) formulas using pre-existing GBD 2021 relative risks and WHO/JMP sanitation coverage estimates with BAPC/ARIMA projections and multiplicative decomposition—without any primary empirical sample, quasi-experimental policy/intervention comparator, observational exposure-outcome estimation, systematic literature search, or empirical coding of legal/governance rules—none of the standard risk-of-bias checklists apply (chosen_tool = NONE). Methodologically, while the global decomposition framework usefully illustrates how rural population aging offsets mortality declines, inferential validity is constrained by (1) undocumented exclusion of 50 GBD / 16 WHO countries for missing or outlier data, (2) reliance on national rather than rural-specific age distributions and diarrheal mortality rates, (3) assuming time- and space-invariant GBD relative risks from 2000 to 2030, (4) absence of any empirical measurement of sanitation law or governance despite the paper's title, and (5) noticeable internal reporting inconsistencies in text and figure captions (e.g., unit errors and mislabeling sanitation as 'air quality' in Figure 5).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Soriano et al. (2016) present a single-jurisdiction descriptive institutional and policy case study of the 2014-2015 water supply crisis in the Metropolitan Region of São Paulo (MRSP), evaluating hydrological scarcity, multi-level water governance, and utility interventions through the five-stage UNISDR disaster risk management framework (prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured non-validated judgment), the study's primary strengths lie in its jurisdictional specificity (D2: strong) and rich institutional contextualization (D10: strong) across municipal land-use, basin-committee (PCJ, CEIVAP), state utility/regulator (SABESP, DAEE), and federal/interstate (ANA, Paraíba do Sul) tiers, alongside explicit conceptual thresholds for crisis onset and rationing versus supply breakdown (D3, D4: adequate). However, empirical rigor is substantially limited (weak across D1, D5-D9, D11-D13): the paper lacks a Methods section, documentary search or selection criteria, primary data verification, causal counterfactuals, analytical separation of meteorological drought severity from governance failures or co-occurring interventions (tariff bonuses vs. network pressure reduction), reflexivity regarding the authors' federal agency affiliations, and complete archival citations (including unindexed survey statistics and local C:/ drive URLs).

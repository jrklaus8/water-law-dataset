## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Cunha and Borja (2018) present a doctrinal-institutional policy evaluation paired with a descriptive administrative case study of federal Growth Acceleration Program (PAC 1 and PAC 2) basic sanitation investments in the State of Bahia (2007–2014). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (structured non-validated judgment), the study demonstrates strong legal source accuracy and rich institutional contextualization—grounding its critique in the 1988 Federal Constitution, Lei nº 11.445/2007, Lei nº 11.079/2004, Lei nº 12.305/2010, and Plansab (2013)—and clearly specifies its state-level jurisdictional boundaries across Bahia's 417 municipalities. However, its empirical rigour is limited by several methodological weaknesses: (1) the contract universe is restricted to 109 state-managed contracts signed between 2007 and 2012 under SEDUR, Embasa, and Conder, potentially omitting 2013–2014 PAC 2 signings and federal PAC funds transferred directly to autonomous municipalities; (2) core contract data rely on unpublished internal SEDUR spreadsheets alongside conflicting federal portals without documented reconciliation or coding rules, and internal figures show unexplained discrepancies (e.g., R$ 0.91 billion reported for PAC 2 Bahia overall versus R$ 1,017.08 million across water and sewerage components); (3) promised analyses of municipal HDI and financial execution/disbursement performance do not appear in the Results; (4) bivariate frequency tabulations lack multivariate control for confounding institutional factors such as Embasa's municipal concession footprint; and (5) there is no reflexivity discussion regarding the lead author's role within SEDUR.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Britto, Maiello, and Quintslr (2018) present an exploratory, diachronic qualitative case study of water supply inequalities in the Rio de Janeiro Metropolitan Region (RMRJ), drawing on three years of field research, key-informant interviews, archival/planning documents, and descriptive IBGE census tabulations (1940-2010). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits clear research aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative socio-technical approach (Q2: Yes), a well-justified historical-diachronic single-case design (Q3: Yes), clear and well-contextualized findings (Q9: Yes), and substantial conceptual and policy value in showing how nominal network coverage hides severe peripheral supply intermittency shaped by centralized infrastructure path-dependence and institutional governance (Q10). However, methodological reporting of the primary field component raises moderate concerns (4 Can't tell items and 1 No item): informant recruitment and sample composition are not transparently reported (Q4: Can't tell), interview guides, recording/transcription, and resident data-collection procedures are omitted (Q5: Can't tell), researcher reflexivity is absent (Q6: No), ethical review and consent procedures for named interviewees are unreported (Q7: Can't tell), and qualitative coding/analytic procedures are not described (Q8: Can't tell).

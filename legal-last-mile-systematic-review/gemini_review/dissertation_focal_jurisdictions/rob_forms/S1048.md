## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Heller (2007) is a single-country legal-institutional and historical macro-case synthesis examining Brazil's basic sanitation sector from the 1970s PLANASA state-utility concession regime through the 1988 Federal Constitution, the 2003 creation of the Ministry of Cities/SNSA, the Public Consortia Law (Law No. 11,107/2005), and the National Sanitation Law (Law No. 11,445/2007), illustrated with descriptive secondary statistics from IBGE (2000a, 2000b), Brazil (2006), Costa (2003), and Rezende (2005). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework as a structured non-validated judgment, the paper is strong on jurisdictional specificity (D2) and institutional/political-economy context (D10), and adequate in identifying key legal milestones (D1) and distinguishing nominal network coverage from water treatment safety and wastewater interception/treatment outcomes (D3, D4). However, as the author explicitly cautions on p. 142 (PDF p. 3), the article is only a 'preliminary assessment' with no Methods section, no systematic literature or data selection protocol (D5, D6), weak measurement and citation precision for secondary statistics (e.g., citing IBGE 2000a for 1991-2003 trends and omitting methodological details for Figure 1's risk tiers; D7, D11), and no counterfactual identification or statistical confounding control to isolate PLANASA's institutional effects from regional HDI, municipal size, and income inequality (D8, D9). Accordingly, it provides rich institutional background but carries high risk of bias if treated as causal empirical evidence.

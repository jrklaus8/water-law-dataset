## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Murphy et al. (2015) report an exploratory sequential mixed-methods study of drinking water system operators in Canadian First Nations communities, combining semi-structured qualitative interviews in 8 southern Ontario First Nations communities (March–August 2014) with a subsequent closed-ended descriptive survey of 79 First Nations water operators at an annual conference in British Columbia (October 2014). Under the MMAT (2018), the study demonstrates strong mixed-methods rationale and integration (5.1–5.3 = Yes): findings from the exploratory Ontario interviews directly informed the B.C. questionnaire, and both components are coherently synthesized to illuminate how the federal AANDC 80:20 operation-and-maintenance funding split, Band Council budget control, and compensation disparities affect operator certification and retention. However, per MMAT guidance that overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component, the study carries substantial methodological concerns (5.5 = No). In the qualitative strand, recruitment suffered severe selection loss (only 8 of 45 listed / 28 reachable southern Ontario communities agreed to participate, partly due to operators' fear of repercussions), interviews were hand-recorded on paper without audio recording, analytic coding is described in a single sentence without transparent thematic procedures, and Ontario findings are reported without verbatim quotes (1.3, 1.4 = Can't tell). In the quantitative descriptive strand, the B.C. sample relied on convenience sampling at an 'Operational Excellence' conference that likely under-sampled operators from communities lacking training/travel support (4.2 = No), the total conference denominator and survey response rate were not reported (4.4 = No), the unvalidated questionnaire yielded ambiguous responses (e.g., 3 respondents checking both satisfied and unsatisfied; 4.3 = Can't tell), and minor reporting defects appear in the published results (salary counts of 50 + 16 + 33 = 99 exceeding N = 79 on PDF p. 7, and missing category axis labels on Figures 3 and 5).

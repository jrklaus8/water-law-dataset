## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Walters et al. (2012) present an analytical cross-sectional comparison of multi-barrier drinking water protection risk scores between First Nation reserve drinking water systems (n = 143 systems across 137 communities) and non-First Nation municipal/township systems (n = 54 responding communities) in Ontario. Methodological strengths include a detailed, accurate institutional-legal characterization of Ontario's post-Walkerton statutory regime versus the fragmented federal policy/funding framework on reserves, objective GIS mapping relative to Ontario's 19 source water protection regions, and near-census administrative coverage of Ontario First Nation water systems via 2005 and 2007 Access to Information records. However, overall risk of bias is rated as High concern under the JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional Checklist due to four compounding limitations: (1) substantial potential for volunteer/non-response selection bias in the municipal comparator survey, for which neither the sampling frame, invitation criteria, nor response rate is reported; (2) differential outcome ascertainment and temporal mismatch, comparing external multi-day AANDC audits from 2005/2007 against self-reported questionnaires completed by municipal staff at an unspecified later date; (3) pervasive item-level missingness and documented scoring ambiguity in the First Nations AANDC records (30/143 systems missing health parameter data and 128/143 missing volume sufficiency data); and (4) purely descriptive unadjusted counts and visual map inspection without statistical testing, missing-data sensitivity analysis, or formal stratification/adjustment for sharp imbalances in system population size and remoteness.

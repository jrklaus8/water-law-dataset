## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This primary qualitative case study (van Karnenbeek, Salet, and Majoor 2021) examines citizen self-organised wastewater management in the Oosterwold urban development experiment (Almere, the Netherlands) by triangulating doctrinal legal research, qualitative analysis of municipal planning and contractual documents, 3 pilot interviews, and 19 semi-structured key actor interviews, supplemented by secondary water board compliance counts. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits clear research aims, an appropriate and well-justified case study design, multi-source data collection, exceptionally strong specification of European, national, and municipal legal rules (verified with lawyers during interviews), and a lucid, nuanced synthesis of findings (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q9 = Yes; strong Q10 value). Methodological concerns are moderate (4 'Can't tell' items: Q4, Q6, Q7, Q8), stemming from incomplete reporting of interviewee sampling and stakeholder breakdown, minimal discussion of researcher-participant reflexivity beyond lawyer review of doctrinal analysis, sparse ethics reporting (anonymity noted without consent/ethics review details), and limited transparency in qualitative coding and voice representation (no direct resident quotations appear in the empirical sections, and water quality outcomes rely on summary pass/fail counts from water board inspections rather than primary pollutant measurements).

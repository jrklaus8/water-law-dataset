## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This longitudinal qualitative field study (appraised using the CASP Qualitative Checklist; Moderate methodological concern: 4 Yes, 4 Can't tell, 1 No across Q1-Q9, plus strong substantive value on Q10) monitored a purposive stratified sample of 17 volunteer households in Campina Grande, Brazil over 6 monthly visits (October 2014-April 2015), spanning 1 month prior to and 5 months during CAGEPA's municipal water rationing. Key strengths include a clear conceptual and institutional framing, a prospective longitudinal design capturing pre- and post-rationing routines, and effective triangulation of semi-structured interviews with direct household observation and descriptive storage/consumption tracking—which crucially revealed that low-income households reporting 'no impact' were in fact practicing severe, naturalized water deprivation and unsafe container storage. Key methodological limitations include unreported volunteer recruitment and selection procedures within strata (Q4), lack of detail on interview guides, recording/transcription, and coding/analytic rigor (Q5, Q8), absence of ethics committee/informed consent reporting beyond anonymity (Q7), and lack of researcher reflexivity regarding repeated in-home visits (Q6).

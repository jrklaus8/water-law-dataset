## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Baijius and Patrick (2019) present a participatory qualitative multiple-case study of source water protection (SWP) planning across six First Nation communities in the Canadian Prairies (Treaties 4, 5, 6 and 7), interpreted through Robbins's political ecology framework of degradation and marginalization. Methodological strengths under the CASP Qualitative Checklist include clearly articulated research aims (Q1), an appropriate qualitative participatory case design linking local infrastructure vulnerabilities to colonial water governance (Q2, Q3), an explicit and thoughtful researcher positionality statement addressing settler-colonial power dynamics (Q6), and a clear synthesis of recurring on-reserve water risks—household water cisterns, backyard sewage 'shoot-outs' and unlined landfills—and their roots in federal Indian Act control and jurisdictional exclusion (Q9, Q10). However, transparency on empirical execution is limited across four CASP domains rated 'Can't tell': within-community participant recruitment and sample sizes are omitted (Q4); meeting counts, data recording/note-taking procedures and exact risk-matrix scoring rules are unreported (Q5); formal Research Ethics Board approval, community research protocols and informed consent procedures are not stated despite working with Indigenous communities (Q7); and qualitative analytic/coding and validation procedures are not described (Q8). Overall methodological concern is therefore rated Moderate (4 Can't tell / 0 No items).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

McCullough and Farahbakhsh (2012) present a well-structured primary qualitative grounded theory study examining how federal policies, programs, and processes (PPP) shape First Nations drinking water infrastructure delivery in Ontario, based on 13 semi-structured face-to-face interviews with 16 First Nations technical practitioners conducted in late 2009. Assessed with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, 8 of the 9 closed-response items (Q1-Q5, Q7-Q9) are rated 'Yes' and 1 item (Q6, researcher reflexivity) is rated 'Can't tell' (0 'No' items), yielding an overall judgment of moderate methodological concern. Key strengths include a clear policy rationale, purposeful inclusion of diverse technical roles across northern, southern, road-accessible, and remote First Nations contexts, REB approval with explicit attention to Indigenous research ethics, systematic NVivo 8 open/axial/selective coding, preliminary participant feedback validation, and post-analysis documentary triangulation. Methodological limitations include the absence of explicit researcher positionality/reflexivity reporting (Q6), lack of a published interview guide or discussion of thematic saturation and coder independence, three paired interviews that could introduce peer influence, voluntary tradeshow/snowball recruitment that may over-select frustrated practitioners, and reliance on practitioner perceptions (without federal/INAC interviews or direct site-level water quality measurements) to characterize institutional behavior and infrastructure outcomes.

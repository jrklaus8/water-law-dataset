## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

High methodological concern. Although Section 3 labels the research a 'mixed-methods design' using an adapted Nominal Group Technique (NGT), the actual empirical execution omitted qualitative idea generation, round-robin discussion, and qualitative data analysis, consisting solely of a single cross-sectional, self-administered online 0–5 Likert questionnaire completed by a convenience sample of 54 respondents in Maceió, Brazil (11 researchers, 11 utility professionals, and 32 service users) following a non-systematic narrative mapping of ESG strategies from the literature. Key methodological weaknesses under the JBI Analytical Cross-Sectional checklist include: (1) non-probability convenience recruitment without a defined sampling frame, response rate, or verified inclusion criteria, alongside an unexplained discrepancy between defining g2 as managers at state-owned CASAL (Section 3.3) and reporting 54.5% federal public sector affiliation with no state-sector category among experts (Section 4.1); (2) reliance on an unvalidated, double-barreled Likert instrument measuring subjective agreement rather than objective sanitation, fiscal, or ESG maturity outcomes, plus an internal count discrepancy (claiming 35 strategies in Section 3.2 while listing 34 in Tables 1–3 and Appendix A); (3) absence of confounding identification or control across stakeholder groups, with Scenario 2's degree-and-experience weighting conflating stakeholder role with academic credentials; (4) purely descriptive arithmetic mean comparisons on ordinal Likert items in small subsamples (n = 11 per expert group) with zero dispersion statistics or inferential hypothesis tests; and (5) selective non-reporting of collected questionnaire items (including baseline ESG familiarity Q10–Q11 and open/perception questions Q15–Q20) and item-level 'not applicable' exclusions.

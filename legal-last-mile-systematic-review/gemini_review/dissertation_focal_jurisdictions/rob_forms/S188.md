## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Borja (2014) is an institutional-legal policy case study and descriptive public-finance analysis examining Brazil's federal basic sanitation policy and investment trajectory (focused primarily on 2003-2010, with historical budget comparisons from 1996-2009) under the Ministry of Cities and Law 11.445/2007. Although the author describes the study as 'quali-quantitativa' (PDF Page 6, p. 437), there is no primary qualitative empirical data collection (such as interviews, focus groups or systematic documentary coding) and no quasi-experimental comparator or statistical regression model; instead, the paper combines macro-institutional and political-economy analysis of statutory and budgetary rules with descriptive tabulations and maps of federal OGU, FGTS-Caixa and FAT-BNDES sanitation allocations largely synthesized from the author's Ministry of Cities Panorama report (Borja, 2011). Assessed under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured non-validated judgment with no numeric composite score), the study demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity (D2) and institutional contextualization (D10), alongside adequate legal source accuracy (D1), financing exposure and fiscal outcome definitions (D3, D4) and administrative registry coverage (D5-D7, including disclosure of 2.7% missing municipal identifiers in Caixa contracts). However, causal identification (D8), confounding control (D9), standalone replication potential (D11), coding transparency for budget classifications and shifting year windows (D12) and researcher reflexivity regarding Ministry of Cities funding and normative positioning (D13) are weak, limiting the study to descriptive evidence of distributive misalignment between federal financing rules and regional/municipal sanitation deficits.

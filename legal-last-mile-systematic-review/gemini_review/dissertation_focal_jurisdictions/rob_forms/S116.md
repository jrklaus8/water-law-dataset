## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Study S116 (Castleden et al., 2017) is a secondary systematic realist review synthesizing 63 peer-reviewed and grey-literature documents (published 2000–2015 from a 1979–2015 search) on integrative Indigenous and Western knowledge approaches to water research and management in Canada. Appraised with AMSTAR 2, overall confidence in the review's results is Critically Low due to four critical flaws (Q2, Q7, Q9, Q13) alongside four non-critical weaknesses (Q6, Q8, Q10, Q16). Methodological strengths include co-design of eligibility criteria and extraction questions with a National Advisory Committee of Indigenous knowledge holders and Western water experts and a national Water Gathering, searching three academic databases/engines plus multiple Canadian grey-literature repositories with librarian-refined search strings (Table 1; Q4 = Partial Yes), two-stage duplicate independent screening of Scopus and Web of Science records (Q5 = Yes), and rich realist discussion of regional, treaty, and disciplinary heterogeneity (Q14 = Yes). However, the review lacked a pre-registered written protocol with deviation tracking (Q2 = No); restricted eligibility to English in bilingual Canada without methodological justification (Q4 = Partial Yes); partitioned data extraction across three team members rather than extracting in duplicate (Q6 = No); omitted both a list of excluded full-text studies (Q7 = No, with an unexplained numerical gap between 226 retrieved academic citations and 104 screened records) and any study-by-study table or complete bibliography identifying the 63 included documents (Q8 = No); conducted no risk-of-bias or methodological rigour appraisal of included studies (Q9 = No) and therefore could not account for primary study quality in synthesis (Q13 = No); did not report individual study funding sources despite extracting them (Q10 = No); and omitted a conflict-of-interest declaration despite review co-authors authoring primary studies in the field (Q16 = No).

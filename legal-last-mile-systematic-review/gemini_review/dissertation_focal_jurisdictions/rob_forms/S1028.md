## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Snider (2004) is a single-case socio-legal and institutional-historical case study examining the May 2000 E. coli drinking water disaster in Walkerton, Ontario (7 deaths, 2,321 illnesses), the subsequent nine-month Walkerton Public Inquiry (2000-2001), and the resulting O'Connor Report (2002) and Safe Drinking Water Act (2002). Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured non-validated judgment), the study's core strengths lie in its jurisdictional specificity (D2: strong) and deep institutional contextualization (D10: strong) of municipal PUC operations, Ministry of the Environment budget/staff cuts, laboratory privatization without mandatory adverse-result reporting, non-enforcement of inspection deficiencies, and the procedural mechanics of the Public Inquiry. Legal source accuracy, exposure/outcome definitions, treatment of co-occurring physical and local operational confounders (extreme rainfall, fractured bedrock, 1978 well grandfathering, 20 years of operator misconduct), source verifiability via dated Inquiry transcripts, and theoretical reflexivity are adequate (D1, D3, D4, D9, D11, D13). However, the article lacks a formal Methods section, offering weak sampling transparency (D5), case/excerpt selection rules (D6), operational measurement transparency (D7), formal causal identification (D8), and documentary coding transparency (D12).

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Patrick, Grant, and Bharadwaj (2019) present a primary qualitative community-based participatory research (CBPR) case study of a five-stage source water protection planning process at Muskowekwan First Nation in Saskatchewan, Canada. Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study exhibits high methodological concern as an empirical research report (1 'No' on Q8 and 4 'Can't tell' ratings on Q4, Q5, Q6, and Q9). Strengths include a clear research aim (Q1), an appropriate participatory planning methodology and culturally adapted five-stage design (Q2, Q3), and explicit ethical governance under OCAP principles, University of Saskatchewan REB approval, Chief and Council oversight, and Indigenous protocols (Q7). However, empirical documentation is weak: total participant counts and individual recruitment mechanics across the six open council-chamber workshops are omitted (Q4); methods for recording qualitative data (e.g., audio recording, transcripts, or field notes) are unreported (Q5); critical researcher positionality and influence are not examined beyond procedural trust-building (Q6); no qualitative coding or thematic analysis procedure is described (Q8); and interpretive findings regarding cultural identity, empowerment, and 'reconnection with the water spirit' are asserted without a single verbatim participant quote, while infrastructure and water security outcomes rely on subjective consensus risk rankings and champion-assessed cistern repairs without microbiological or chemical water testing (Q9).

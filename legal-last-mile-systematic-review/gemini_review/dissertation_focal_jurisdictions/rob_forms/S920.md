## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Barde (2017) conducts a municipal-level panel difference-in-differences (DiD) evaluation combined with multinomial probit propensity-score kernel matching across rural Brazil (2000-2010) to test whether small-scale piped water systems implemented and operated by community water user associations (WUAs) expand household piped-water access more than systems implemented and operated by local governments. Methodological strengths include nationwide administrative and census coverage, effective covariate balancing (<20% standardized bias; Figure 1, Table 11), an intra-municipal urban piped-water placebo test ruling out municipality-wide governance shocks (Table 5 col. 2), verification of parallel 1990-2000 pre-treatment trends (Table 5 col. 6), a Nijman-Verbeek test for split-municipality attrition (Table 5 col. 7), and regional disaggregation confirming positive WUA effects in Southern Brazil where donor umbrella programs were rare (Table 5 col. 3). Overall ROBINS-I risk of bias is rated Moderate (with No information on D7 due to the absence of a pre-analysis plan). Key limitations preventing a Low rating are: (1) non-randomized project selection over an eight-year window without an exogenous natural experiment, alongside bundled state/donor technical and financial support in Northeast umbrella schemes (CENTRAL, SISAR/KfW, PROSAR) and inclusion of concurrent 2000-2008/2010 change variables in the preferred propensity-score specification (D1/D4); (2) coarse municipal-level exposure coding from PNSB 2000 and 2008 waves that pools mixed WUA-plus-local-government municipalities into +assoc, omits exact project timing within 2000-2008, and leaves 2008-2010 provider changes unobserved before the 2010 Census (D3); (3) ~14% listwise covariate attrition among project municipalities in matched models (D2/D5); and (4) outcome measurement restricted to household network connection rates without water quality or service reliability data.

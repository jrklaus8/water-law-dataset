## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

This urban geography and social-policy case study uses a convergent mixed-methods ('quali-quantitativa') descriptive design to examine the implementation of the federal 'Saneamento Para Todos' water supply expansion program in the peripheral Águas-Lindas neighborhood straddling Belém and Ananindeua (Pará, Brazil). Its empirical strengths lie in combining tract-level IBGE 2010 Census microdata, Power BI database linkage, GIS/satellite spatial mapping across six subdivisions, public procurement scrutiny (COSANPA Bidding Notice No. 009/2017, five contract amendments, and 43 monthly investment status spreadsheets from 2019-2022), and dated field photography (July and November 2022) documenting intra-neighborhood infrastructure disparities and street disruptions from delayed pipe-laying works. However, appraised under MMAT (2018), overall methodological quality is limited by serious weaknesses in both strands. In the qualitative strand, although the authors invoke Magnani's ethnographic approach and use field notes and photography, zero interviews or community observations were conducted with residents or COSANPA social-work staff, leaving key claims about resident distrust, refusal of water connections, clandestine alternatives, and lack of participatory Trabalho Técnico Social unsubstantiated (1.2, 1.4, 1.5 rated 'No'). In the quantitative descriptive strand, the authors rely on 2010 Census data to represent a rapidly growing periphery in 2022, compare nominal 2010 per-capita incomes (R$ 214-R$ 364) directly against nominal 2023 CadÚnico (R$ 650) and water tariff (R$ 24.90/10m³) benchmarks without inflation adjustment, and subtract 2020 planned connections from 2010 unserved household counts without accounting for a decade of housing growth (4.2, 4.3 rated 'No'). Following MMAT 2018 guidance (no composite numeric score; overall quality bounded by the weakest components), the study presents high methodological concern for claims regarding household affordability and resident attitudes, while providing credible descriptive evidence of historical spatial inequality and administrative/construction delays.

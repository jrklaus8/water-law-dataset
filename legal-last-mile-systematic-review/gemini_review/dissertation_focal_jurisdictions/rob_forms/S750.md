## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Pascual Sanz, Schouten and Hantke-Domas (2011) present a qualitative comparative case study of consumer inclusion mechanisms in water tariff setting across three Western European utilities representing distinct regulatory archetypes: Vitens in the Netherlands (self-regulation), Agbar/EMA in Barcelona, Spain (bipartite negotiation) and Scottish Water/WICS in Scotland (independent regulation). Appraised with the CASP Qualitative Checklist, the study demonstrates clear aims (Q1: Yes), an appropriate qualitative comparative approach (Q2: Yes), a coherent analytical framework adapted from Stern & Holder (1999) across 15 transparency, participation and accountability indicators (Q3: Yes), and a nuanced presentation of findings that distinguishes formal legal provisions from practical barriers (Q9: Yes). However, methodological transparency for the primary interview component is weak (4 'Can't tell' items and 1 'No' item): informant sample sizes, recruitment procedures, interview protocols, ethical safeguards and qualitative coding/triangulation rules for assigning ordinal L/M/H scores are omitted from the published article (Q4, Q5, Q7, Q8: Can't tell), and researcher reflexivity is absent despite first-author affiliation with Vitens Evides International, funding from Suez Environment and acknowledged guidance from Agbar staff (Q6: No). Cross-case causal attribution is also inherently constrained by national legal-historical confounding across n=3 single-jurisdiction cases and by measuring procedural mechanisms rather than downstream tariff or service outcomes.

## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Gouveia et al. (2026) conduct an exploratory convergent mixed-methods field study examining intramunicipal 'hydrosocial scarcity' among households formally connected to the public water supply network in São Gonçalo (Rio de Janeiro Metropolitan Region, Brazil), framed against Brazil's universalisation mandate (Laws No. 11,445/2007 and No. 14,026/2020) and the UN human right to water. Using 2010 IBGE Census tract data and municipal sanitation records, the authors purposively selected two contrasting connected neighbourhoods—low-income Jardim Catarina (n=45 households in one census tract) and higher-income Mutondo (n=46 households in one census tract)—and administered semi-structured household questionnaires/interviews alongside field observations in November–December 2021. Appraised with the MMAT (2018) using the Quantitative Descriptive (4.1-4.5) block, the study's main strengths lie in its clear research question, strong rationale for mixing survey frequencies with qualitative resident accounts, effective narrative integration across service dimensions, and explicit exploration of apparent paradoxes (such as pump- or cistern-driven feelings of water security amidst severe network rationing). However, per MMAT 2018 guidance that overall quality cannot exceed the weakest component, there is high-to-moderate methodological concern: (1) sampling is non-probabilistic ('typical case sampling') and restricted to one accessible/safe census tract per neighbourhood (visiting 4 of 6 streets and 3 of 5 alleys) based on 2010 Census frames without reported household selection rules or refusal/non-response rates; (2) the questionnaire instrument is unpublished and unvalidated, relying solely on subjective self-report without physical hydraulic or water-quality verification; (3) quantitative analysis uses raw percentages without inferential tests, confidence intervals, or confounding control, and contains minor text/percentage reporting errors (PDF pp. 9 and 13); and (4) qualitative transcription, coding, thematic analysis, reflexivity, and formal research ethics approval are unreported.

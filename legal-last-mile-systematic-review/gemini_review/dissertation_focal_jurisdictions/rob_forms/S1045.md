## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Jiménez, Cortobius, and Kjellén (2014) conduct a secondary scoping/thematic literature review of peer-reviewed articles on indigenous peoples and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WaSH), searching SciVerse Scopus with paired keywords (782 records) and screening abstracts down to 185 included articles grouped into six thematic areas (PDF pp. 3-4). Appraised with AMSTAR 2, overall confidence in the review's results is Critically Low due to multiple critical domain flaws: there is no a priori protocol or registration (Q2), the search relied on a single bibliographic database without supplementary grey-literature or reference searches (Q4), excluded studies are not listed individually and a substantial portion of the 185 selected articles—such as non-'key' traditional ecological knowledge papers—are omitted from narrative synthesis without an inventory (Q7, Q8), and no risk-of-bias assessment of included primary studies was performed (Q9) or incorporated into the interpretation of findings (Q13). Study selection and thematic coding were also not reported as performed in duplicate (Q5, Q6). While useful as an exploratory thematic map highlighting the literature's skew toward resource conflicts in high-income settler states over basic WaSH service delivery in low-income settings, its synthesis cannot support high-confidence causal or comparative conclusions.

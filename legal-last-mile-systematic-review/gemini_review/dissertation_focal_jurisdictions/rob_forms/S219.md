## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Aquino and Ledo (2023) pair a stylized two-period dynamic regulation model with an unbalanced longitudinal municipality-year panel econometric analysis (2002-2021; N = 1,142 descriptive observations, N = 983 in regressions) testing whether municipal and state election cycles reduce real water/sanitation tariff adjustments across Brazilian utilities regulated by subnational agencies. Appraised with ROBINS-I, the empirical evaluation is at Serious overall risk of bias. Four domains reach Serious risk: (1) confounding (D1), because municipal and state elections occur nationwide on fixed 4-year calendar cycles and the models omit general year fixed effects and macroeconomic/input-cost controls capable of separating nationwide year shocks from electoral pressure; (2) participant selection (D2), because utilities enter only if online normative tariff-adjustment resolutions were found and, as the authors acknowledge, municipalities with severe political interference that enacted zero adjustments over the study period are completely excluded; (3) missing data (D5) and (4) outcome measurement (D6), because post-entry gaps where contacted regulatory agencies did not reply were imputed as 0% nominal tariff adjustments—conflating non-response with real tariff freezes—alongside 13.9% complete-case attrition from missing SNIS sewage data. Classification of election indicators, co-interventions, and incomplete/erroneous table and text reporting (including duplicate row labels in Table 3, a sign typo in Table 1, omitted control coefficients, and a misstated Hausman test rule) introduce additional Moderate concerns in D3, D4 and D7. Finally, although framed around welfare and network expansion, the empirical regressions test only real tariff adjustments rather than actual sanitation expansion outcomes.

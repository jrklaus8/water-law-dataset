## 3. Overall Narrative Summary

Narzetti and Marques (2021) present a national institutional and legal-historical case study of water and sanitation services (WSS) reforms in Brazil (1964-2020), applying the Policies, Institutions, and Regulation (PIR) and 'isomorphic mimicry' frameworks to argue that Brazilian WSS reforms are robust de jure (especially Law no. 11.445/2007) yet ineffective de facto, particularly for rural and informal peri-urban populations. Appraised under the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework (as a structured, non-validated qualitative judgment rather than a numeric score), the study demonstrates strong jurisdictional specificity (D2) and institutional contextualization (D10), and adequate legal source identification (D1, marred by lack of article pinpoints and two statutory year typos in Section 6) and conceptual exposure framing (D3). However, empirical and methodological rigor is weak across sampling transparency (D5), selection process (D6), measurement transparency (D7), outcome definition (D4), coding transparency (D12), replication potential (D11), causal identification (D8), and treatment of confounding (D9), with researcher reflexivity not assessable (D13). The paper contains no Methods section, reports no systematic document selection or coding rubric for its claimed inspection of 73 regulatory agencies' laws, websites, and management reports, relies on aggregate cross-sectional secondary coverage statistics (MDR 2019; IBGE 2020), and asserts isomorphic mimicry as the primary cause of WSS access deficits without comparative or counterfactual testing against acknowledged demographic, fiscal, and land-tenure confounders.

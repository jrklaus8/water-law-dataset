## 2. Substantive Methodological Patterns Observed Directly in Full Texts

Because Claude's September 2026 risk-of-bias batch pass (`RISK_OF_BIAS.md` §4) appraised most `CASP`, `MMAT`, `JBI`, `ROBINS-I`, and `Legal Framework` studies mechanically from sparse `extraction_database.csv` columns (producing `"Can't tell"` on 6 of 10 CASP items and 5 of 5 MMAT integration items across nearly the entire corpus), reading the **full source PDFs** in Task D yields three concrete methodological findings for the PI:

1. **Full-text reading resolves most `"Can't tell"` artefacts in `CASP` and `MMAT`, exposing real methodological weaknesses instead of missing-extraction blanks:**
   - Across qualitative (`CASP`) and mixed-methods (`MMAT`) studies read in full text, recruitment strategies (`CASP Q4`), data-collection procedures (`CASP Q5`), ethics approvals (`CASP Q7`), and mixed-methods rationale/integration (`MMAT 5.1–5.3`) **are** assessable directly from the Methods sections—whereas researcher reflexivity (`CASP Q6`) and formal divergence handling between quantitative and qualitative strands (`MMAT 5.4`) are genuinely absent (`No` or `Can't tell`) in most primary papers.
2. **Cluster-randomization Domain `1b` (`RoB 2` cluster variant) matters substantively:**
   - Both randomized trials in the readable sample (`S057` Kozole et al. 2023; `S085` Gaikwad & Thomas 2026) randomize clusters (villages / informal settlement clusters) rather than independent households, making Domain `1b` (timing of participant identification/recruitment relative to cluster allocation) essential to evaluate recruitment bias.
3. **Studies fitting `NONE` of the listed tools (`RISK_OF_BIAS.md` §1 gap confirmed independently):**
- **`S175`** (Bellaubi F, Pahl-Wostl C (2017). Corruption risks, management practices, and per…): `Deterministic game-theoretic agent-based simulation model (ABM) parameterized and calibrated using secondary summary scores from five previously published urban case studies in Kenya and Ghana` — This article develops and runs a deterministic computational simulation—an agent-based model (ABM) in NetLogo v4.1.3 built on game-theoretic payoff matrices and bounded-rational learning equations—parameterized and calibrated against pre-existing aggregate ordinal scores from prior publications (Bellaubi and Visscher 2014, 2016; GII 2011; TIK 2011). It reports no primary empirical human-subjects sampling, survey/cohort data collection, quasi-experimental policy evaluation, primary qualitative fieldwork, mixed-methods primary integration, systematic literature search, or doctrinal/jurimetric analysis of primary legal texts. Under RISK_OF_BIAS.md §1, forcing a primary empirical checklist or the Legal Institutional Evidence Appraisal Framework onto a computational simulation model is inappropriate, so NONE is selected.

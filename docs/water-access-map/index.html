Recognition

Is access to water framed as a right?

NLNetherlandsCourt decisionStatute or regulation

The Act's own text states no individual right. The government's note to the 2018 amendment of the disconnection regulation, as quoted by the Hague Court of Appeal (2024), says the cabinet recognises access to water as an important human right and that the Act gives the right to drinking water through the connection duty. The court itself held that the treaty-based right to water has no direct effect, but that 'minimum core obligations' of it may be enforceable, and ordered protection for children on the general duty of care.

BRBrazilCourt decisionStatute or regulationTitle or contents only

Framed as a right in practice, but without a retrieved constitutional text. A federal statute names the "direito humano à água potável" as the aim of a fund the Executive may create, subject to budget (Lei 14.898/2024). Courts describe water supply as an essential public service "necessary to satisfy a fundamental right" (TJDFT 2018), treat it as a good "indispensable to life" under the Constitution's dignity and social-rights provisions (a 2019 headnote), and the STJ (2023) left in place an order against a concessionaire resting on a constitutional provision, with water by tanker truck in the meantime.

BRBrazilBill, not lawCourt decision

Federal law, as the STJ states it (2025), requires urban buildings to be connected to the public network (Lei 11.445/2007, art. 45). A connection is made on the user's request and does not recognise ownership (a Federal District decree, as quoted). But in the Federal District new water connections to lots in irregular subdivisions made after 2011 are prohibited, and two TJDFT decisions (2015, 2019) upheld the water company's refusal. The 2015 court held that the service being essential does not by itself compel a connection.
